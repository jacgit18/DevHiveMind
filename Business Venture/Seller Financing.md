In the context of buying a business, **seller financing** (also called **owner financing**) is when the seller of the business acts as the lender, allowing the buyer to purchase the business through installment payments rather than securing a traditional loan from a bank or financial institution.

### **How Seller Financing Works in a Business Purchase**

1. **Negotiation of Terms** – The buyer and seller agree on the loan amount, interest rate, down payment, repayment period, and any collateral.
2. **Purchase Agreement & Promissory Note** – A legal contract outlines the terms of the financing, including repayment schedules and default consequences.
3. **Due Diligence & Transition** – The buyer ensures the business’s financials are sound and may negotiate training or transition support from the seller.
4. **Payments to Seller** – The buyer makes agreed-upon payments to the seller over time while operating the business.
5. **Final Ownership Transfer** – Once the loan is fully paid, the seller releases any remaining claims on the business (if applicable).

### **Why Seller Financing is Common in Business Sales**

- **Easier Financing** – Banks may be reluctant to fund small business purchases, so seller financing provides an alternative.
- **Shows Seller Confidence** – If the seller is willing to finance, it signals that they believe in the business’s profitability.
- **Wider Buyer Pool** – Buyers who may not qualify for a traditional loan can still purchase the business.
- **Tax Benefits for Seller** – Spreading out payments over time can reduce the seller’s immediate tax burden.

### **Typical Seller Financing Terms for Businesses**

- Down payment: 10-50% of the purchase price
- Loan term: 3-7 years (sometimes longer)
- Interest rate: 5-10% (varies by market and agreement)
- Collateral: Often includes business assets, personal guarantees, or liens

### **Checklist for Evaluating Seller Financing in a Business Purchase**

#### **1. Assess the Business Itself**

✅ **Financial Statements Review** – Examine at least 3 years of profit/loss statements, balance sheets, and tax returns.  
✅ **Cash Flow Analysis** – Ensure the business generates enough revenue to cover loan payments and operational costs.  
✅ **Valuation** – Get an independent business valuation to ensure the asking price is fair.  
✅ **Industry & Market Conditions** – Assess trends, competition, and potential risks.

#### **2. Evaluate the Seller's Financing Offer**

✅ **Down Payment Requirement** – Determine the percentage required upfront (typically 10-50%).  
✅ **Loan Terms & Interest Rate** – Compare with traditional financing options (common interest rates: 5-10%).  
✅ **Repayment Schedule** – Check if it aligns with expected business cash flow.  
✅ **Balloon Payments** – Identify if a large lump sum is required at the end of the term.  
✅ **Prepayment Terms** – See if early payoff is allowed without penalties.

#### **3. Verify Legal & Contractual Details**

✅ **Promissory Note** – Ensure a legally binding agreement with clear repayment terms.  
✅ **Security & Collateral** – Understand if the seller is taking a lien on business assets or requiring personal guarantees.  
✅ **Non-Compete Agreement** – Ensure the seller won’t open a competing business after the sale.  
✅ **Default & Repossession Terms** – Know the consequences if payments are missed.

#### **4. Conduct Due Diligence on the Seller**

✅ **Verify Business Ownership & Debts** – Ensure there are no undisclosed liabilities or legal disputes.  
✅ **Check UCC Filings** – See if the business has outstanding loans that could affect ownership transfer.  
✅ **Assess Seller’s Motivation** – A motivated seller might offer better terms.

#### **5. Plan for a Smooth Transition**

✅ **Training & Handover Agreement** – Negotiate a transition period where the seller helps you learn operations.  
✅ **Key Employee Retention** – Ensure staff and management will stay post-sale.  
✅ **Supplier & Customer Relationships** – Verify contracts and key relationships are stable.

### **Sample Seller Financing Contract Terms & Negotiation Strategies**

#### **1. Sample Seller Financing Contract Terms**

**1. Loan Amount & Purchase Price**

- **Total Purchase Price:** $500,000
- **Down Payment:** $100,000 (20%)
- **Financed Amount:** $400,000

**2. Interest Rate & Term**

- **Interest Rate:** 7% annually
- **Loan Term:** 5 years (60 months)
- **Monthly Payment:** $7,920 (amortized over 5 years)

**3. Collateral & Security**

- **Business Assets:** Seller retains a lien on equipment, inventory, and intellectual property until full repayment.
- **Personal Guarantee:** Buyer agrees to be personally liable for the loan.
- **UCC-1 Filing:** Seller files a security interest in the business assets to protect against default.

**4. Payment Schedule & Structure**

- **Equal Monthly Payments:** $7,920
- **Balloon Payment (if applicable):** $100,000 due at the end of year 5
- **Prepayment Terms:** Buyer can prepay without penalties after 2 years.

**5. Default & Remedies**

- **Missed Payment Terms:** Buyer has 30 days to cure any missed payments.
- **Repossession Rights:** If buyer defaults, seller can take back the business assets.
- **Legal Recourse:** Seller can sue for unpaid amounts or enforce personal guarantee.

**6. Seller Involvement Post-Sale**

- **Transition Period:** Seller provides 3 months of operational training.
- **Consulting Agreement:** Seller remains available for advisory support for 12 months at $100/hour.
- **Non-Compete Clause:** Seller cannot start or work for a competing business within 50 miles for 5 years.

### **2. Negotiation Strategies for Seller Financing**

#### **For Buyers:**

✅ **Push for Lower Down Payment** – Start by offering 10-15% and negotiate up.  
✅ **Secure Favorable Interest Rates** – Use SBA loan rates as leverage (typically 5-7%).  
✅ **Request a Grace Period** – Ask for 3-6 months of interest-only payments at the start.  
✅ **Avoid Balloon Payments if Possible** – If required, negotiate a refinance option.  
✅ **Ensure Seller Has Skin in the Game** – If the seller believes in the business, they should offer flexible terms.

#### **For Sellers:**

✅ **Get a Larger Down Payment** – Aim for at least 20-30% to reduce risk.  
✅ **Charge a Higher Interest Rate** – Justify it as compensation for the risk of financing.  
✅ **Retain a Lien on the Business** – File a UCC-1 to secure the debt with business assets.  
✅ **Limit Buyer’s Ability to Resell** – Require full repayment before the business can be transferred.  
✅ **Include a Personal Guarantee** – Protect yourself if the buyer defaults.

The **7 Ds**—Death, Disease, Dullness, Departure, Disagreement, Distress, and Divorce—are often used to evaluate **the motivations and stability** of the seller in a business transaction, particularly in seller financing. These factors can indicate why the seller is offering financing and help you assess potential risks and negotiation leverage.

---

### **Evaluating the 7 Ds in Seller Financing**

1. **Death** – Has there been a recent death of the owner or key stakeholders?
    
    - Is the business being sold due to the owner's passing?
    - Are heirs involved, and do they want a quick sale?
    - Does the estate need liquidity, making them more flexible on terms?
2. **Disease** – Is the owner selling due to health issues?
    
    - Are they trying to exit quickly due to personal illness?
    - Is there a risk that business continuity or key knowledge will be lost?
    - Will they stay on for transition support, or is this a full exit?
3. **Dullness** – Is the owner simply bored or burned out?
    
    - Has the business stopped growing because the owner lost interest?
    - Are there untapped growth opportunities that a motivated buyer could pursue?
    - Could you negotiate better terms since they just want out?
4. **Departure** – Is the owner relocating or leaving the industry?
    
    - Are they moving away for family, retirement, or another job?
    - Is their departure planned or abrupt?
    - Could this impact the local business relationships or supplier network?
5. **Disagreement** – Are there internal conflicts forcing the sale?
    
    - Are there partnership disputes that make seller financing risky?
    - Are co-owners pushing for a sale due to disagreements?
    - Will the seller be difficult to work with post-sale if they are financing part of the deal?
6. **Distress** – Is the business struggling financially?
    
    - Is the business under financial pressure (e.g., debt, declining revenue)?
    - Are suppliers or lenders pressuring them to sell?
    - Are they offering seller financing because they can’t find a cash buyer?
7. **Divorce** – Is a personal breakup affecting the sale?
    
    - Is the owner selling due to divorce and needing cash quickly?
    - Is there a risk of legal complications from a spouse’s stake in the business?
    - Does this urgency make them more open to negotiation?

### **How to Use the 7D Framework in Seller Financing Negotiations**

When discussing a seller-financed deal, you can subtly probe these areas to assess risk and opportunity:

- **"What’s prompting the sale?"** _(Detect Death, Disease, Departure, Divorce, or Distress)_
- **"Have you been actively involved in the business lately?"** _(Gauge Dullness or Disagreement)_
- **"Are you looking for a quick exit, or do you want to stay involved in some capacity?"** _(Identify Departure motivations & flexibility in financing terms)_
- **"How has the business been performing in the last 12-24 months?"** _(Spot potential Distress issues before committing to financing terms)_

A seller's motivation can impact their **willingness to negotiate, structure financing, and provide transition support.** If they are selling due to **distress or disputes**, it may signal **higher risk**, while **departure or dullness** may present an opportunity to acquire a business with growth potential.
