## What are the eligible expenses defined for HSAs in the IRS

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) defines eligible expenses for Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) as those related to medical care. Eligible expenses include:

Diagnostic services: such as medical tests and exams, X-rays, and other diagnostic procedures.

Treatment and prevention: including treatment for physical or mental illnesses, accidents, or injuries, as well as preventive care, such as vaccines and screenings.

Hospital services: including inpatient and outpatient hospital care, as well as related services, such as laboratory tests and nursing care.

Prescription drugs: including both prescription and over-the-counter medications.

Medical equipment and supplies: such as wheelchairs, crutches, and other medical equipment, as well as hearing aids and other related supplies.

Vision and dental care: including vision exams, eyeglasses, contact lenses, and dental exams, as well as related services and supplies.

Long-term care: including costs related to nursing home care, assisted living, and home health care.

The IRS provides a comprehensive list of eligible expenses, and it's important to keep in mind that the list may change from time to time, so it's important to stay up-to-date on the current rules. Withdrawals from an HSA for non-eligible expenses are subject to income tax and a 20% penalty if made prior to age 65.





## How to use HSA effectively

Here are some tips to effectively use a Health Savings Account (HSA):

Use it for eligible expenses: Make sure you're using your HSA funds for qualified medical expenses as outlined by the IRS.

Take advantage of tax benefits: Contributions to an HSA are tax-deductible and withdrawals for eligible expenses are tax-free.

Invest HSA funds: Consider investing a portion of your HSA funds in a diversified portfolio to potentially earn higher returns, but make sure you balance that with keeping enough liquid funds available to pay for eligible expenses.

Track expenses and receipts: Keep track of your HSA contributions and withdrawals, as well as receipts for eligible expenses, in case you need to provide proof to the IRS.

Plan for long-term expenses: Consider how you might use your HSA funds in the future, such as for healthcare costs in retirement.

Research plan options: Research different HSA plans to find one that fits your specific needs and compare costs, investment options, and account management tools.

Use a budget: Incorporate your HSA contributions and withdrawals into your overall budget to ensure you are making the most effective use of your HSA funds.
