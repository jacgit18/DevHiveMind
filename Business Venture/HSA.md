The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) sets annual contribution limits for Health Savings Accounts (HSAs). The current contribution limits for the tax year 2022 (for individuals with self-only coverage under an HSA-qualified high-deductible health plan) are $3,650. For those with family coverage under an HSA-qualified high-deductible health plan, the contribution limit is $7,300. Additionally, individuals age 55 or older may make catch-up contributions of up to $1,000.

If you have your own **HSA** (Health Savings Account) and healthcare plan that is separate from your employer, you can still contribute to the HSA pre-tax, but the process is slightly different from when it’s done through employer payroll deductions. Here's how you can set it up and contribute pre-tax:  
  
### 1. **Establish Your HSA**  
- **Open an HSA Account**: If you don't already have one, you’ll need to open an HSA account with a qualified provider. Many banks, credit unions, and financial institutions offer HSAs.  
- **Ensure You're Eligible**: You must be enrolled in a **high-deductible health plan (HDHP)** to qualify for an HSA.  
 
### 1. **How to Contribute Pre-Tax If It's Not Through Employer Payroll**:  
  
Since you’re not using payroll deductions from your employer, you’ll need to set up **direct contributions** to your HSA. Here's how to do that pre-tax:  
  
#### **Option 1: Make Pre-Tax Contributions Through Your Employer's Payroll (if applicable)**  
- Even if you're not on your employer's health plan, some employers may still allow you to contribute to an HSA via payroll deductions for tax benefits.  
- To set this up, you would:  
1. **Talk to HR or Benefits Coordinator**: Ask if they allow **HSA contributions** from non-employees or for individuals with their own healthcare plan.  
2. **Fill Out Enrollment Forms**: If your employer allows it, you’ll need to complete forms specifying how much you want to contribute.  
3. **Automatic Deduction**: Your employer will deduct the contribution from your paycheck before taxes, lowering your taxable income.  
  
#### **Option 2: Contribute Directly to Your HSA (If Employer Doesn’t Offer Pre-Tax Payroll Deductions)**  
- If your employer doesn’t offer HSA contributions via payroll (or you don’t want to go that route), you can still contribute directly to your HSA.  
- **Pre-tax Benefits via Tax Filing**:  
- While contributions are made with after-tax money (i.e., you contribute directly from your bank account), **you can still deduct those contributions** on your tax return.  
- When you file your taxes, you’ll report your HSA contributions on IRS Form 8889 and deduct them from your taxable income, reducing the amount of taxes you owe.  
- This means you’re effectively still contributing "pre-tax" since the IRS will reduce your taxable income by the amount you contributed to the HSA.  

- **Step-by-Step**:  
1. **Contribute to Your HSA**: You deposit funds into your HSA directly, either via a one-time transfer or monthly contributions.  
2. **Track Contributions**: Keep records of all your HSA contributions, as you’ll need them for tax purposes.  
3. **File Taxes**: When filing your taxes, use **Form 8889** to report the contributions and deduct them from your taxable income.  
4. **Max Contribution Limits**: Be sure to stay within the annual contribution limits set by the IRS.  
  
---  
  
### 3. **Contribution Limits for 2024**  
- **Individual Coverage**: Up to **$4,150** per year.  
- **Family Coverage**: Up to **$8,300** per year.  
- **Catch-Up Contributions**: If you're **55 or older**, you can contribute an extra **$1,000** per year.  

---  
  
### 4. **Why Contribute Pre-Tax to an HSA?**  
- **Tax Savings**: Contributing pre-tax (either via payroll deductions or tax deduction) lowers your taxable income for the year.  
- **Tax-Free Growth**: Any money in the HSA can grow tax-free if you invest it.  
- **Tax-Free Withdrawals**: As long as you use the funds for qualified medical expenses, withdrawals are tax-free.  

---  
  
### **Summary**  
- If you have your own HSA and healthcare plan separate from your employer, you can still contribute pre-tax by **deducting contributions on your tax return** (rather than through payroll).  
- You'll need to track your contributions and report them when filing taxes to lower your taxable income.  
- If your employer allows it, you might also be able to set up payroll deductions even if you're not on their health plan, which would automatically be deducted pre-tax.  

By contributing pre-tax dollars to your HSA, you benefit from reduced taxable income while saving for medical expenses in a tax-efficient way.


## HSA & Transactions

When you incur a medical expense, such as a bill from a doctor or a purchase at a pharmacy, you can choose to pay for it out of pocket, even if you don’t have enough money already in your HSA. After paying, you can deposit that same amount into your HSA later if you wish.

This approach allows the money in your HSA to continue growing through investments since you're not withdrawing it immediately. You can reimburse yourself for those out-of-pocket medical expenses, including pharmacy costs, at any time in the future, as long as you keep the receipts.
  
  
### HSA-Eligible Over-the-Counter Items

#### **1. Medications**

- Pain relievers (e.g., ibuprofen, acetaminophen, aspirin)
- Allergy medications (e.g., antihistamines, decongestants)
- Cold and flu remedies (e.g., cough syrup, throat lozenges)
- Antacids and heartburn relief (e.g., Tums, Prilosec)

#### **2. First Aid Supplies**

- Bandages, gauze, and medical tape
- Antiseptic creams and ointments (e.g., Neosporin)
- Thermometers
- Hot and cold packs

#### **3. Health and Diagnostic Products**

- Blood pressure monitors
- Glucose monitors and testing strips
- Pulse oximeters

#### **4. Personal Health Items**

- Sunscreen (SPF 15 or higher)
- Lip balm with SPF
- Menstrual care products (e.g., pads, tampons, menstrual cups)
- Condoms and contraceptive devices

#### **5. Skin and Wound Care**

- Hydrocortisone cream
- Acne treatments
- Eczema and psoriasis creams

#### **6. Baby and Child Care**

- Infant thermometers
- Breast pumps and supplies
- Diaper rash creams

### **Important Notes**

1. **Prescription Requirements:**  
    Some items, like vitamins or orthopedic shoes, may require a doctor’s note or prescription to qualify.
    
2. **Gray Areas:**  
    Items like fitness trackers or massage equipment are only eligible if explicitly prescribed for a medical condition.
    
3. **Record Keeping:**  
    Save all receipts and documentation to support eligibility in case of an IRS audit.
    

---

**Ineligible Items (Common Misconceptions):**

- General toiletries (e.g., toothpaste, shampoo, deodorant)
- Cosmetic products (e.g., makeup, anti-aging creams)
- Snacks, drinks, or vitamins (unless prescribed for a medical condition)
- Non-medical items like cleaning supplies or hand sanitizer (unless part of a medical prescription).
