Yes, **$200k+ annual income** is roughly the threshold where spending **$15k/year (~$1,250/month) on restaurants and premium food** aligns with financial best practices—assuming you're optimizing for **both enjoyment and long-term wealth**.

But let’s refine this further, because "making sense" depends on **two key angles**:

1. **Affordability** (Can you spend it without hurting your finances?)  
2. **Opportunity Cost** (Are you *losing* money by *not* spending it?)

---

### **1. The Affordability Threshold (~$150k–$200k+)**

At **$200k/year gross income**:
- **Take-home pay** (after taxes, 401k, etc.): **~$120k–$140k/year** ($10k–$12k/month)
- **$15k/year on food = ~12% of take-home pay** (within the **10–15% "comfortable" range** for food spending)
- **At $150k/year**, it’s **~15–18% of take-home pay**—doable, but starting to strain other goals

**Below $150k?** It’s **not financially optimal** unless:
- You have **no debt** and **high savings already**
- You’re **explicitly prioritizing food over other luxuries** (travel, cars, etc.)

---

### **2. When You’re *Losing Money* by *Not* Spending It**

This applies if **skipping $15k on food costs you more in lost opportunities**. Examples:

#### **A. Business/Income Generation**
- **Networking Dinners**: If a $200 meal lands you a $20k client, it’s a **10,000% ROI**
- **Content Creation**: Food blogging, reviews, or social media where dining out = revenue
- **High-Earning Time Trade-Off**: If cooking saves you $1k/month but costs you $5k in lost billable hours, **you’re losing $4k by being frugal**

**Income needed to justify this: $250k+**

#### **B. Health & Longevity ROI**
- If eating out means **healthier meals** (e.g., no time to cook junk food), leading to:
  - Fewer medical costs (chronic diseases are expensive)
  - Higher productivity (better energy/focus)
- **Example**: A heart attack costs **~$750k+** in lifetime earnings—spending $15k/year on quality food could be **cheap insurance**

**Income needed: $150k+**

#### **C. Relationship ROI**
- If dating over dinners leads to a **high-value partnership** (dual incomes, shared goals), the $15k could pay off long-term
- **But**: If it’s just fun with no ROI, it’s a **sunk cost**

---

### **Final Verdict: When $15k on Food Makes Sense**

| **Income**      | **Makes Sense?**      | **Why?**                                               |
|-----------------|-----------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| **<$100k**      | ❌ No                 | Strains budget; better to cook + selective dining      |
| **$100k–$150k** | ⚠️ Maybe              | Doable if you sacrifice other luxuries                |
| **$150k–$200k** | ✅ Yes (Comfortable)   | Fits 10–15% food budget without stress                |
| **$200k+**      | ✅ Yes (Optimal)       | Negligible impact on wealth-building                  |
| **$250k+**      | ✅ **Yes (ROI-Driven)** | If food spending generates income/health benefits     |

---

### **Key Exception: The "You’re Losing Money" Case**

You’re **only "losing money" by not spending $15k on food if**:
1. It **directly increases your income** (e.g., biz meals, content)
2. It **saves you from bigger costs** (health, time, opportunity loss)

**Otherwise, it’s just lifestyle spending—not an investment.**

---

### **Actionable Takeaway**
- **Under $150k?** Cap dining at **$500–$800/month**, invest the rest
- **Over $200k?** Spend freely if it brings joy or ROI
- **Track ROI**: If food isn’t making/saving you money, reallocate
