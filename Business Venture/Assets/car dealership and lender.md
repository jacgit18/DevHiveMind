To determine if a car dealership is also the lender, you can inquire about the financing options they offer and ask specific questions about the loan arrangement. Here are some steps you can take to gather information:  
  
1. Research the dealership: Look for information about the dealership online, visit their website, or call their customer service to gather information about their financing options. Some dealerships may explicitly mention that they provide in-house financing or act as the lender.  
  
2. Ask about financing: When you visit the dealership or contact them, inquire about the available financing options. Specifically, ask who provides the financing for the vehicles they sell. If they mention that they offer in-house financing or provide loans directly, it indicates that they are likely acting as the lender.  
  
3. Review loan documents: If you decide to proceed with financing through the dealership, they will present you with loan documents to sign. Carefully review these documents, including the loan agreement and any disclosures provided. The lender's information should be clearly specified in these documents. Look for indications that the dealership is the lender or if there is another financial institution involved.  
  
4. Obtain a copy of the title: If the dealership holds the title to the car, ask them about the process for obtaining the title after the loan is paid off. If they mention that they are the ones who hold the title and will release it to you once the loan is satisfied, it suggests that they are acting as the lender.  
  
If you want to avoid dealing with the dealership as the lender, you can explore alternative financing options from banks, credit unions, or online lenders. Pre-approval from an external lender before visiting the dealership can help you bypass their financing and negotiate from a stronger position.  
  
It's always recommended to carefully read and understand any contracts or loan agreements before signing them. If you have any doubts or concerns, seeking advice from a legal professional or financial advisor can provide valuable insights specific to your situation.
