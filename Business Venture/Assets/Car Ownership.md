- $50,000 for bodily injury or death per accident  
- $10,000 for property damage per accident

3. **Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Coverage**: While not required by law in New York, uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage provides protection in case you're involved in an accident with a driver who doesn't have insurance or doesn't have enough insurance to cover your damages. ## Highway Tips
When you rent a car and drive on the New York State Thruway, the tolls can be handled in a few different ways:  
  
1. **Rental Car Company's Toll Program**: Most rental car companies offer an electronic toll payment program. You'll be charged a daily fee plus the tolls you incur. This is often the easiest option but can be more expensive due to service fees.  
  
2. **Bring Your Own E-ZPass**: If you have your own E-ZPass, you can use it in the rental car. Make sure to add the rental car's license plate to your E-ZPass account.  
  
3. **Pay by Mail**: If you don't have an E-ZPass, the toll system will capture an image of the license plate and send a bill to the rental car company, which will then charge you. This can include additional processing fees.  

### Steps to Use Your Own E-ZPass in a Rental Car:  
1. **Check with the Rental Company**: Confirm that using your own E-ZPass is allowed.  
2. **Add License Plate**: Add the rental car's license plate to your E-ZPass account temporarily.  
3. **Turn Off Rental Transponder**: If the rental car has a built-in toll transponder, ensure it's turned off to avoid double billing (ask the rental company how to do this).
  
It's important to check with your rental car company for specific details on how they handle toll payments and any associated fees.

Passing vehicle is allowed when it is safe to do so and legal according to traffic laws. Here are some common scenarios and guidelines for passing safely:  
  
1. **Single Lane Roads:**  
- Passing on single lane roads is generally not permitted unless there are designated passing zones or the road markings indicate that passing is allowed.  
- If you encounter slow-moving traffic on a single lane road, you may need to wait for a passing zone or until the road widens to safely pass.  
  
2. **Two Lane Roads:**  
- On two lane roads with one lane of travel in each direction, passing is typically allowed when there is a broken yellow line on your side of the road and it is safe to pass.  
- Ensure that there is no oncoming traffic, and check your mirrors and blind spots before attempting to pass.  
- Do not pass if there are solid yellow lines on your side of the road, as this indicates that passing is not allowed due to limited visibility or other hazards.  
  
3. **Multi-Lane Roads:**  
- On roads with multiple lanes of travel in each direction, passing is generally allowed in any lane designated for through traffic.  
- Use the left lane (or other designated passing lanes) to pass slower-moving vehicles in the right lane. Signal your intention to pass and check for other vehicles before changing lanes.  
- Avoid passing on the right unless there are multiple lanes of travel in the same direction and the vehicle you are passing is making a left turn or is traveling significantly below the speed limit.  
  
4. **Highways and Freeways:**  
- On highways and freeways with multiple lanes of travel in each direction, passing is common and allowed in any lane designated for through traffic.  
- Use the leftmost lanes to pass slower-moving vehicles in the right lanes. Be mindful of faster-moving traffic and yield to vehicles traveling at higher speeds.  
  
5. **Safe Passing Conditions:**  
- Only pass when it is safe to do so and there is sufficient visibility and space to complete the maneuver.  
- Check your blind spots, use your turn signals, and ensure that there is enough distance to pass before returning to your original lane.  
- Be aware of potential hazards such as intersections, curves, hills, and pedestrians before attempting to pass.  
  
In New York City and throughout New York State, these passing guidelines generally apply. However, always be mindful of local traffic laws, road markings, and signs that may indicate specific passing regulations or restrictions. Additionally, use caution and courtesy when passing to ensure the safety of yourself and other road users.

## Car Safety 
Using high beams and dimming the rearview mirror at night are important practices to ensure safe driving visibility and to prevent glare for yourself and other drivers. Here's when and how to use them:  
  
1. **High Beams:**  
- Use high beams when driving on dark, unlit roads with no oncoming traffic or when driving in rural areas with minimal street lighting.  
- High beams provide enhanced visibility by illuminating a longer distance ahead of your vehicle.  
- However, always remember to dim your high beams when approaching oncoming traffic or when driving behind another vehicle. This prevents blinding other drivers with excessive glare, which can be dangerous and distracting.  
- Additionally, dim your high beams when driving in fog, rain, or snow, as the light can reflect off water particles and reduce visibility.  
  
2. **Dimming Rearview Mirror:**  
- Dimming the rearview mirror at night helps reduce glare from headlights of vehicles behind you, improving visibility and reducing eye strain.  
- Most modern cars come equipped with auto-dimming rearview mirrors that automatically adjust to reduce glare.  
- If your vehicle does not have an auto-dimming feature, manually adjust the mirror to its night mode. This is usually achieved by flipping a switch or pressing a button located on the bottom of the mirror.  
- Remember to return the rearview mirror to its normal position during daytime driving to maintain maximum visibility of vehicles behind you.  
- Adjust your mirrors so that you don’t see any of the car's body. This gives you a broader perspective of the surrounding area.
- **Left-side mirror**: Adjust it away from the car to expand your field of view.
- **Right-side mirror**: You can either adjust it the same way or keep it as is to maintain a reference for parking, depending on your preference.

## Gas 
Most cars do not have the capability to directly determine the quality of the gasoline being used. However, modern vehicles are equipped with sensors and systems that monitor various parameters related to engine performance and fuel efficiency. While these systems cannot specifically analyze the quality of the gasoline, they can detect issues or anomalies that may arise from using low-quality or contaminated fuel.  
  
Here are some ways in which a car may indirectly indicate issues related to the quality of gasoline:  
  
1. **Check Engine Light:** If the gasoline being used causes issues with engine performance or emissions, the vehicle's onboard diagnostics system may trigger the check engine light to alert the driver to a potential problem. This could indicate issues such as misfires, fuel system malfunctions, or emissions control system faults.  
  
2. **Fuel System Problems:** Low-quality or contaminated gasoline can lead to problems with the fuel system, such as clogged fuel filters, injector deposits, or fuel pump issues. Symptoms of fuel system problems may include rough idling, hesitation or stumbling during acceleration, or reduced fuel efficiency.  
  
3. **Emissions Testing:** In areas where emissions testing is required, using low-quality gasoline may cause the vehicle to fail emissions tests due to elevated levels of pollutants in the exhaust gases. This could indicate issues with fuel combustion or emissions control systems.  
  
4. **Poor Performance:** Low-quality gasoline may result in reduced engine performance, including decreased power output, slower acceleration, and decreased fuel efficiency. If you notice a significant drop in performance after refueling, it could be a sign of poor-quality gasoline.  
  
While cars themselves cannot directly assess the quality of the gasoline being used, drivers can take steps to ensure they are using fuel from reputable sources and adhere to manufacturer recommendations for fuel quality and octane rating. Additionally, regular maintenance and inspections can help identify and address any issues related to fuel quality or engine performance.

Here are some liability insurance options for cars:  
  
1. **State-Minimum Liability Insurance**: Every state in the U.S. requires drivers to have a minimum amount of liability insurance coverage. These minimum requirements vary by state and typically include coverage for bodily injury liability per person, bodily injury liability per accident, and property damage liability.  
  
2. **Standard Liability Insurance**: Standard liability insurance policies typically provide coverage beyond the state minimums. They offer higher limits of bodily injury and property damage liability coverage, providing greater protection in the event of an accident.  
  
3. **Umbrella Liability Insurance**: Umbrella insurance policies offer additional liability coverage that goes beyond the limits of standard auto insurance policies. They provide an extra layer of protection against liability claims and lawsuits, covering costs that exceed the limits of your primary auto insurance policy.  
  
4. **Non-Owner Car Insurance**: Non-owner car insurance provides liability coverage for drivers who don't own a car but occasionally drive rental cars or borrow vehicles from friends or family. It typically includes bodily injury and property damage liability coverage.  
  
5. **Rental Car Insurance**: Rental car companies offer liability insurance options for renters. This coverage protects against claims for injuries or damage caused to others while driving a rental car. It's usually offered as supplemental liability insurance (SLI) or a liability damage waiver (LDW).  
  
6. **Commercial Auto Insurance**: For businesses that own or operate vehicles for business purposes, commercial auto insurance provides liability coverage for vehicles used in business activities. It typically includes higher liability limits and may also cover employees who drive company-owned vehicles.  
  
7. **Personal Umbrella Policy**: A personal umbrella policy provides additional liability coverage that extends beyond the limits of your auto insurance and other personal insurance policies. It covers liability claims against you for bodily injury, property damage, and other types of personal liability.  
  
When selecting a liability insurance option for your car, consider factors such as coverage limits, deductibles, premiums, and any additional features or benefits offered by the policy. It's essential to choose coverage that provides adequate protection based on your individual needs and financial situation.
