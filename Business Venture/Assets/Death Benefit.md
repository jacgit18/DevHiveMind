# Considerations When Increasing Your Death Benefit on a Life Insurance Policy

#todo/Life/Research
- [ ] When increasing your death benefit on a life insurance policy, there are several key factors to consider, as well as additional policy options:

## 1. Underwriting Requirements

- **Medical Exams**: Most insurers will require a new medical exam or updated health information to assess your current health status.
- **Insurability**: If your health has declined since you first purchased the policy, you may face higher premiums or even be denied an increase.

## 2. Cost of Premiums

- A higher death benefit typically means higher premiums.
- Ensure the increased cost aligns with your budget and long-term financial plans.

## 3. Purpose of the Increase

Consider why you need a higher death benefit, such as:
- Replacing income for dependents.
- Covering new debts (like a mortgage).
- Funding future expenses (e.g., education or estate taxes).

Ensure the additional coverage meets these specific goals.

## 4. Cash Value Impact (For Permanent Policies)

- Increasing the death benefit may impact the cash value growth, as more premiums may be allocated toward the insurance cost rather than the savings portion.
- Some policies allow you to choose between:
  - **Level death benefit**: Keeps the benefit constant.
  - **Increasing death benefit**: Adds the cash value to the payout but increases premium costs.

## 5. Tax Considerations

- If you increase your death benefit significantly and overfund your policy, it may exceed IRS Modified Endowment Contract (MEC) limits.
- If your policy becomes a MEC, loans and withdrawals from the cash value may lose their tax-advantaged status.

## 6. Alternatives to Increasing Coverage

Instead of increasing the death benefit, you could:
- Purchase a separate policy for the additional coverage.
- Add riders (e.g., Paid-Up Additions Rider for whole life).
- Explore term life insurance to supplement permanent coverage at a lower cost.

## 7. Long-Term Financial Goals

Ensure the increased death benefit aligns with your overall financial strategy, including retirement planning, wealth transfer, and estate planning.

Before making changes, review your policy with your insurer or financial advisor to assess the cost, benefits, and impact on your cash value and long-term needs.
