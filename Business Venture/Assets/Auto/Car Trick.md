## Brake torquing
When you press the brake pedal and the accelerator (gas) pedal simultaneously in a vehicle with an automatic transmission, it is known as "brake torquing" or "brake boosting." Brake torquing is a technique used by some drivers to launch a vehicle quickly from a stationary position, particularly in situations like drag racing or quick acceleration scenarios.

By applying the brakes and pressing the gas pedal simultaneously, the driver creates a build-up of torque in the engine. The brake torquing technique allows the engine to rev up to higher RPMs while the vehicle remains stationary, resulting in a faster launch when the brakes are released.

This technique can be effective in certain situations where a quick start is desired, such as in drag racing or when trying to merge into fast-moving traffic. However, it should be noted that brake torquing puts extra stress on the drivetrain and can lead to excessive wear and tear, particularly on the transmission and torque converter. It may not be recommended or suitable for regular driving situations and can potentially cause damage if used improperly or excessively.

It's important to note that brake torquing should be performed with caution and within legal limits, as it may violate traffic laws or regulations in some jurisdictions. Additionally, it's advisable to check your vehicle's owner's manual or consult with a professional to understand any specific considerations or limitations related to brake torquing for your particular vehicle.


## Rev matching
used to smoothly downshift while maintaining control over the vehicle's speed and engine RPM.

![[G1kxF3PdTZOJWwTZsXhZ.gif]]

Here's how the process works:

1. Downshifting: When you're driving a manual transmission vehicle and need to slow down or prepare for a lower gear, you typically need to downshift. Downshifting involves shifting to a lower gear to match the speed of the vehicle with the engine RPM.

> Downshifting after coming to a stop
> 	Unless your vehicle has massive power on tap, it won't accelerate from a stop in high gear. So you will have to downshift anyway to get your car to move. So basically you downshift when you slow down to put your car in a position to speed up again.


2. Holding the clutch: Before downshifting, you press the clutch pedal to disengage the engine from the transmission. This allows you to shift gears without causing grinding or excessive wear on the transmission components.

3. Revving the engine: While the clutch pedal is still depressed, you apply some pressure to the gas pedal to increase the engine RPM. This is done to match the engine speed to the desired gear before engaging the clutch again.

4. Engaging the clutch: After revving the engine and reaching the appropriate RPM for the lower gear, you smoothly release the clutch pedal while simultaneously releasing the gas pedal. This allows the engine power to transfer to the wheels as the clutch engages, resulting in a smooth downshift.

The purpose of holding the clutch and pressing the gas pedal during downshifting is to synchronize the engine speed with the transmission speed, ensuring a smoother transition between gears. By rev matching, you can reduce wear on the clutch and transmission components, prevent jerking or lurching, and maintain better control over the vehicle's balance and stability during the downshift.

It's important to note that mastering the technique of rev matching takes practice and familiarity with your specific vehicle's clutch and transmission characteristics. Improper rev matching or excessive revving can lead to clutch slippage, premature clutch wear, and potential damage to the drivetrain. If you're unsure about how to properly perform this technique, it's advisable to consult your vehicle's owner's manual or seek guidance from a qualified driving instructor.
