# 💰 Revenue Model Overview

A **revenue model** explains how a business makes money—it defines the **source(s) of income**, **pricing strategy**, and **customer segments** that generate that income.

It’s a core part of a business model and helps answer:  
➡️ _"Who pays you, what do they pay for, how much, and how often?"_

---

## 🔍 Key Components of a Revenue Model

---

### 1. Revenue Streams

These are the different **sources of income** for a business.

**Examples:**
- **Product sales** (e.g., Apple selling iPhones)  
- **Service fees** (e.g., consulting, repairs)  
- **Subscription revenue** (e.g., Netflix, Spotify)  
- **Licensing** (e.g., software or patents)  
- **Advertising** (e.g., Google, Meta)  
- **Commission or transaction fees** (e.g., Airbnb or eBay taking a cut)  
- **Freemium-to-premium upgrades** (e.g., LinkedIn, Dropbox)

---

### 2. Customer Segments

**Who is paying you?**

- **B2C** (Business to Consumer)  
- **B2B** (Business to Business)  
- **B2G** (Business to Government)  
- **Internal cost centers** (in large companies)  

Knowing your segments helps with **pricing strategy** and **lifetime value projections**.

---

### 3. Pricing Strategy

**How much do you charge and how?**

- **Flat-rate pricing** – One standard price (e.g., $9.99/month)  
- **Tiered pricing** – Different levels or bundles (e.g., Basic, Pro, Enterprise)  
- **Usage-based** – Pay for what you use (e.g., AWS, utilities)  
- **Per transaction** – Small fee per sale (e.g., Stripe)  
- **Dynamic pricing** – Price changes with demand (e.g., airline tickets, Uber surge)  
- **Freemium** – Free access to basic features, with paid upgrades

---

### 4. Sales Volume & Frequency

**How often do customers pay, and how much do they spend?**

- **One-time purchase** vs. **recurring revenue**  
- **High volume, low price** (e.g., retail, fast food)  
- **Low volume, high price** (e.g., luxury goods, enterprise SaaS)

---

### 5. Channels

**How do you reach and collect money from your customers?**
