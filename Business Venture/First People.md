The first hires when starting a business depend on the type of business, its goals, and your own strengths and weaknesses. Generally, you'll want to focus on roles that directly contribute to building your product/service, attracting customers, and ensuring smooth operations. Here's a breakdown of who you should consider hiring first:

### **1. Co-founder or Business Partner (if applicable)**

- **Why?** If you don't already have a co-founder, finding someone who complements your skillset (e.g., you're technical, and they excel at sales/marketing) can help split responsibilities and bring additional expertise to the table.
- **Key traits:** Aligned vision, complementary skills, and trustworthiness.

### **2. Core Team Members**

- **Product/Service Developer:** If your business is product-focused (e.g., tech, manufacturing), hire someone to build or refine the product. This could be a software engineer, designer, or technical specialist.
- **Sales or Marketing Expert:** If you already have a product but need customers, a sales or marketing hire is crucial to generating leads and building awareness.
- **Operations Manager:** If your product is ready and you're selling, an operations or logistics hire can streamline the back-end (e.g., supply chain, inventory, fulfillment).

### **3. Administrative Support**

- **Why?** Administrative tasks (scheduling, emails, bookkeeping) can eat up valuable time you should spend on growth. Hiring a virtual assistant or admin early can free you to focus on strategy and execution.

### **4. Accountant or Financial Advisor (Part-Time)**

- **Why?** Keeping your finances in check is critical. Hiring an accountant, even on a contract basis, ensures your books are accurate, taxes are handled, and you’re financially sound from the start.

### **5. Legal Advisor (Consultant)**

- **Why?** You may not need an in-house legal expert early on, but you’ll need legal advice to incorporate, draft contracts, protect intellectual property, and ensure compliance.

### **Who You Might Wait to Hire**

- **HR Specialist:** If your team is small, you can manage hiring and onboarding yourself initially.
- **Customer Support:** If your product is still in development or your initial customer base is manageable, you may not need dedicated support right away.
- **Specialized Roles:** Roles like data analysts, social media managers, or SEO specialists can be added as your business grows and the need arises.

### **Factors to Consider**

- **Budget:** Focus on versatile hires who can wear multiple hats in the early stages.
- **Your Strengths and Weaknesses:** Hire people who fill gaps in your expertise.
- **Business Type and Goals:** A tech startup might prioritize engineers, while a service business might prioritize marketing and customer-facing roles.

Ultimately, your first hires should directly contribute to building, selling, or operating your product/service while allowing you to focus on strategy and growth.


# PreWork
### 🔖 **1. Curate and Compliment Authentically**

- **Bookmark the work and habits** of people you admire — their projects, posts, decisions, or ways of thinking.
    
- Use these as talking points:
    
    - “I saw the way you approached X — I’ve been thinking about that ever since.”
        
    - “That thread you wrote on Y really stuck with me — you have a unique lens.”
        
- **Genuine compliments** create warmth and open the door for future collaboration.
    

---

### 🌱 **2. Cultivate the Relationship**

- **Engage periodically** — not just when you need something.
    
- Respond to what they share. Drop them relevant articles, tools, or frameworks they might enjoy.
    
- If they post about something unfinished or in-progress, say:
    
    - _“If you ever finish that, I’d love to see it.”_
        
    - _“Let me know if you want to brainstorm around this sometime.”_
        

---

### 💡 **3. Share Ideas & Invite Input**

- **Send ideas their way**, even if rough. Show you value their thinking.
    
- Ask for their take or to consult casually:
    
    - “Would love your opinion on this — you’re the first person I thought of.”
        
    - “Mind if I pick your brain on something I’m stuck on?”
        

---

### 🧷 **4. Close the Loop — Fast**

- **If you see someone great, don’t wait.** Make the ask, extend the opportunity, or invite the collaboration early.
    
- Talent moves fast — respect their time and strike while interest is warm.
    

---

### 🔁 **5. Keep Connections Warm, Even When They Leave**

- **People who move on can still be allies** — as connectors, referrals, or future collaborators.
    
- A message like:
    
    - _“Congrats on the move — would love to stay in touch. If you know anyone like you, send them my way.”_  
        keeps doors open.
        

---

### 🧭 **6. High Performers Vet You Too**

- Remember: **good people are evaluating you just as much**.
    
    - Are you organized?
        
    - Do you follow through?
        
    - Do you create clarity and direction?
        
- Be someone **they’re excited to say yes to**.
