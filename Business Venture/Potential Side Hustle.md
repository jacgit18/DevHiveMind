Building websites for free and charging a percentage of revenue is a model known as revenue sharing or performance-based pricing you can also insert ads in your website. Here's how it typically works:

1. **Offering Free Website Development**: As a web developer or agency, you offer to create websites for clients at no upfront cost to them. This can be particularly attractive to startups, small businesses, or individuals who may not have a large budget for website development.

2. **Revenue Sharing Agreement**: Instead of charging a fixed fee for your services, you negotiate a revenue sharing agreement with your clients. This means that you will receive a percentage of the revenue generated by the website over a specified period of time, such as monthly or annually.

3. **Determining Revenue Share Percentage**: The percentage of revenue you charge can vary depending on factors such as the complexity of the project, the expected revenue potential of the website, and the risk involved. It's essential to negotiate this percentage upfront and ensure that it is fair to both parties.

4. **Monitoring and Reporting**: Once the website is live and generating revenue, you'll need to track its performance closely. This may involve implementing analytics tools to monitor traffic, conversions, and revenue generated. Regular reporting to your clients will help maintain transparency and trust in the partnership.

5. **Continuous Optimization**: To maximize revenue and ensure the success of the revenue sharing model, you'll need to continuously optimize the website. This may involve A/B testing, SEO improvements, conversion rate optimization, and other strategies to drive more traffic and increase conversions.

6. **Legal Considerations**: It's crucial to have a solid contract in place that outlines the terms of the revenue sharing agreement, including the percentage of revenue, payment schedule, and duration of the agreement. Consulting with a legal professional to draft or review the contract is advisable to protect both parties' interests.

Overall, the revenue sharing model can be a win-win for both you as the developer and your clients, as it aligns your interests and incentivizes you to create high-performing websites that drive revenue growth. However, it's essential to carefully consider the risks and benefits of this approach and ensure clear communication and transparency throughout the partnership.
