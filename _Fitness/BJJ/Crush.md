### **Elbow and Arm Position in Inside Control**

- **Keep your elbows up** when inside control, positioning them **at the center of your opponent's body**, aligned with the **centerline of their spine**.
- **Be cautious**: This alignment can expose you to an **arm triangle choke**, so stay aware of the pressure and avoid overcommitting.
- **Control their shoulder** next to your head, especially the one they're using to apply pressure, to prevent them from improving their position.

---

### **Half Guard Smash**

- **Half guard** is where **smashes** typically happen.
- Focus on **control grips** (especially near the opponent's head) and **hooking** their leg to maintain position.
- Use **T-Rex arms** to keep them from grabbing your head while you control the far-side arm, specifically the one they might use to attack your head.

---

### **Submission Focus**

- **Guillotines**: Only attempt them from the bottom position, and avoid other submissions that might compromise your defense unless you feel comfortable.
- Against larger opponents, **prioritize post grips** over collar or lapel grips, as they offer better control.

---

### **Framing and Control**

- **Frame with your feet** to create space and maintain posture.
- The next level of control involves using **your knees** for additional framing or adjustments.
- Avoid **separating your arms**, as this will open you up to attacks like armbars or sweeps. **Frame your hips** and control them efficiently.

---

### **Key Points to Remember**

- Stay mindful of **shoulder pressure** and the risk of submissions like arm triangles.
- **T-Rex arms** and leg hooks are key to controlling the opponent’s head and arms.
- When focusing on submissions, pick your battles and don’t risk being exposed while attacking.
- Control your frames with feet and knees to prevent your opponent from advancing or passing.
