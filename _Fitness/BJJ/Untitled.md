In a **real-world scenario** without a gi (e.g., MMA, street defense), your guard must prioritize **distance management**, **minimizing striking power**, and **improving your position quickly**. The following guards are most effective in mitigating punches, kicks, knees, and elbows:

---

### **1. Closed Guard (With Overhooks and Wrist Control)**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Keeps the opponent close, making it difficult for them to generate power for strikes.
    - Overhooks, wrist control, or head control neutralize their ability to punch.
- **Key strategies:**
    - Pull their posture down by controlling their head or arm.
    - Use overhooks to trap their arms and reduce striking threats.
    - Actively look for sweeps or submissions to avoid staying static (e.g., armbar or triangle choke).

---

### **2. Knee Shield Half Guard (Z-Guard)**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Keeps the opponent at a distance with your shin acting as a barrier.
    - Limits the effectiveness of punches and elbows.
    - Provides an opportunity to create space and stand up if needed.
- **Key strategies:**
    - Use your bottom leg to trap their leg, limiting their movement.
    - Control their wrist or bicep to block strikes.
    - Transition to full guard or get back to your feet when possible.

---

### **3. Butterfly Guard**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Keeps the opponent close, reducing their striking range.
    - Allows for off-balancing (sweeps) and transitions to dominant positions.
- **Key strategies:**
    - Stay seated and keep your head tight to their chest to avoid elbows.
    - Use hooks under their legs to control their movement.
    - Elevate and sweep them to gain top control or stand up.

---

### **4. Modified Open Guard (Feet on Hips or Biceps)**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Maintains distance and neutralizes their ability to punch effectively.
    - Controls their posture by using your legs and grips on their wrists.
- **Key strategies:**
    - Push against their hips with your feet to maintain distance.
    - If they throw punches, use your feet to push off and create space to stand up.
    - Transition to sweeps (e.g., push sweep) if they try to close distance.

---

### **5. De La Riva Guard (Modified for No-Gi)**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Controls the opponent's leg and disrupts their balance, making it hard to generate striking power.
    - Allows for sweeps or transitions to more dominant positions.
- **Key strategies:**
    - Use your leg to hook their lead leg and off-balance them.
    - Control their wrist to block strikes while you work on sweeps.

---

### **6. 50/50 Guard**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Entangles the opponent's legs, limiting their ability to throw effective strikes.
    - Forces them to focus on balance rather than striking.
- **Key strategies:**
    - Use your hands to control their free leg and keep their posture low.
    - Transition to leg locks or sweeps to escape dangerous positions.

---

### **7. Double Butterfly Hooks (Elevator Guard)**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Prevents them from posturing up to throw punches or elbows.
    - Can quickly transition to sweeps or stand-up escapes.
- **Key strategies:**
    - Keep your head tucked close to their torso.
    - Use your hooks to elevate and off-balance them.

---

### **Guards to Transition From**

- **Don’t stay static:** If you’re staying in one guard for too long in a real-world scenario, you risk being overwhelmed by strikes. Use these guards to quickly **sweep, escape, or improve your position.**

---

### **Additional Tips for Real-World Scenarios:**

1. **Protect your head:** Always maintain hand or arm frames to block punches.
2. **Create space:** Use your legs and hips to push the opponent away whenever possible.
3. **Escape to standing:** Look for opportunities to transition to your feet and disengage.
4. **Control posture:** Keep your opponent low to prevent strikes from gaining momentum.
5. **Avoid flashy moves:** Focus on simple, high-percentage techniques that prioritize safety and control.

These guards are tailored to mitigate the threat of strikes and ensure you can transition out of dangerous positions effectively.

### Guards to Avoid

- **Deep Half Guard:** Leaves your face exposed to downward strikes.
- **Inverted Guard:** Vulnerable to stomps or downward punches.
- **X-Guard:** Effective for sweeps but leaves your head exposed if not managed carefully.

In a **real-world scenario** where strikes are a concern, sweeps are vital for transitioning from a defensive position on your back to a more dominant position (like mount or top control). Sweeps should be **high-percentage**, quick to execute, and designed to off-balance your opponent while keeping you safe from punches, kicks, or knees. Below are the **best sweeps** for such scenarios:

---

### **1. Hip Bump Sweep**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Uses your hips to generate power, making it hard for the opponent to defend if they’re postured up and trying to strike.
    - Can transition directly to mount, where you have more control.
- **How to do it:**
    - From closed guard, sit up while controlling their wrist or elbow on one side.
    - Post your opposite hand on the mat for stability.
    - Drive your hips up and to the side, rolling them over.

---

### **2. Scissor Sweep**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Simple and works well if the opponent is postured and attempting to strike.
    - Keeps you protected while creating a strong off-balancing motion.
- **How to do it:**
    - From closed guard, transition to an open guard with one shin across their torso and the other leg on the mat.
    - Pull their upper body forward with a collar or wrist grip.
    - Kick your top leg diagonally while sweeping their base leg with your bottom leg.

---

### **3. Flower Sweep (Pendulum Sweep)**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Controls their posture and uses their weight against them, especially if they’re reaching to punch.
    - Takes advantage of momentum and can transition directly into mount.
- **How to do it:**
    - From closed guard, underhook their leg on one side while pulling their arm on the opposite side.
    - Swing your free leg in a pendulum motion to generate momentum.
    - Roll them over and follow into mount.

---

### **4. Butterfly Sweep (Hook Sweep)**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Prevents strikes by keeping the opponent’s posture broken.
    - Works well when seated in butterfly guard and can lead to a dominant position.
- **How to do it:**
    - From butterfly guard, underhook one arm and grab their wrist or shoulder.
    - Use your butterfly hook (leg under their thigh) to elevate and sweep them to the side.

---

### **5. Lumberjack Sweep**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Exploits the opponent’s weight distribution, especially if they’re standing to strike.
    - Puts you in a position to get on top while limiting their striking.
- **How to do it:**
    - If the opponent stands in your guard, grab both ankles and control their posture by pushing your hips up.
    - Pull their ankles while lifting your hips to off-balance them.
    - Follow them to the ground into top control.

---

### **6. Tripod Sweep**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Takes advantage of an opponent standing or posturing up for punches.
    - Maintains distance and off-balances them effectively.
- **How to do it:**
    - From open guard, place one foot on their hip and hook their ankle with your other foot.
    - Grab their wrist or sleeve for control.
    - Push with your foot on their hip while pulling their ankle and wrist to knock them over.

---

### **7. Balloon Sweep (Tomoe Nage)**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Uses your legs to throw the opponent over your head, keeping you safe from strikes.
    - Can lead to standing up or transitioning to a dominant position.
- **How to do it:**
    - From guard, grab their wrist and collar, and place a foot on their hip.
    - Pull them forward and use your foot to lift and flip them over you.
    - Follow up with a scramble to a better position.

---

### **8. Half Guard Sweeps (Knee Tap Sweep or Old School Sweep)**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Neutralizes strikes by entangling the opponent’s legs.
    - Creates opportunities to get on top or back to your feet.
- **How to do it:**
    - From half guard, underhook their far arm and grab their leg or ankle.
    - Drive with your hips while pulling their leg to sweep them over.

---

### **9. Double Ankle Sweep**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Exploits opponents who stand in your guard to rain down strikes.
    - Can quickly neutralize their attacks and put you on top.
- **How to do it:**
    - If they stand in your closed guard, control both ankles with your hands.
    - Push your hips up while pulling their ankles to sweep them.
    - Follow into mount or side control.

---

### **10. Push Sweep (Hip Elevation Sweep)**

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Uses your legs to create space and take advantage of their forward pressure.
    - Keeps their punches or elbows from reaching you.
- **How to do it:**
    - From open guard, place one foot on their hip and the other foot behind their knee.
    - Push with your foot on the hip while pulling their upper body forward to off-balance them.

---

### **Key Principles for Sweeps in a Real-World Setting:**

1. **Control Their Posture:** Keep their upper body down to limit their ability to strike effectively.
2. **Use Timing and Leverage:** Wait for them to overcommit to a strike or movement and exploit their balance.
3. **Transition Quickly:** Don’t hang out on your back; sweep to get on top or create space to stand up.
4. **Protect Your Head:** Always use frames or grips to shield yourself from strikes during the sweep.

Sweeps in a real-world scenario are not just about gaining points—they’re about creating space, limiting damage, and securing a safer position.



When choosing submissions in a **real-world self-defense scenario**, it's crucial to prioritize techniques that:

1. Keep you safe from strikes (punches, kicks, slams).
2. Maintain positional control.
3. Minimize exposure to dangerous counters like being slammed.

Here’s a list of **safer submissions** in such scenarios:

---

### **Chokes (Safe and Effective)**

#### 1. **Rear Naked Choke (RNC)**

- **Why it’s safe:**
    - Keeps you behind the opponent, out of their striking range.
    - No risk of getting slammed since you’re controlling their back.
- **Application:**
    - Secure hooks from back control.
    - Slide one arm under their chin, grip your bicep, and squeeze.

---

#### 2. **Guillotine Choke (Standing or Seated)**

- **Why it’s safe:**
    - Can be applied while sprawling to avoid takedowns.
    - Keeps their head and posture controlled, limiting strikes.
- **Tips for safety:**
    - Apply from standing or seated guard, ensuring your legs are free to sprawl.
    - Avoid pulling guard if slams are a risk.

---

#### 3. **Arm Triangle Choke (From Mount or Side Control)**

- **Why it’s safe:**
    - Performed from dominant positions (top control).
    - No exposure to strikes when properly locked.
- **Application:**
    - Trap their arm against their neck and apply pressure using chest and shoulder.

---

#### 4. **Triangle Choke (Modified for Safety)**

- **Why it’s relatively safe:**
    - Neutralizes their ability to strike while locking their head and arm.
    - Safer when controlled from a **high guard** to avoid ground-and-pound.
- **Tips for safety:**
    - Maintain tight control of their posture to prevent them from posturing up or slamming you.
    - Use an angle to ensure the submission is effective quickly.

---

### **Joint Locks (Prioritize Safety)**

#### 5. **Kimura (From Guard or Side Control)**

- **Why it’s safe:**
    - Controls their arm, limiting their ability to strike.
    - Safer from top side control or guard when you’re controlling their posture.
- **Tips for safety:**
    - Apply pressure quickly to avoid prolonged struggle.
    - Be ready to transition if the opponent tries to slam you.

---

#### 6. **Straight Armbar (Mounted or Side Control)**

- **Why it’s safe:**
    - Effective from dominant positions where you’re less likely to get hit or slammed.
    - Keeps you in control of the arm and limits their ability to punch.
- **Tips for safety:**
    - Avoid armbars from closed guard in a self-defense scenario, as they expose you to strikes.

---

#### 7. **Americana (Top Mount or Side Control)**

- **Why it’s safe:**
    - Performed from dominant positions that limit the opponent’s striking options.
    - Quick submission to incapacitate the opponent.
- **Application:**
    - Pin their wrist to the mat, isolate their arm, and apply pressure to the shoulder joint.

---

### **Leg Attacks (Safer Options)**

#### 8. **Straight Ankle Lock**

- **Why it’s safe:**
    - Allows you to stay at a distance while targeting their lower body.
    - Can be performed without exposing your face to strikes.
- **Tips for safety:**
    - Use this as a quick submission; don’t linger in the position.

#### 9. **Outside Ashi Garami for Heel Hook**

- **Why it’s safer than inside leg positions:**
    - Keeps their other leg farther away, reducing the risk of kicks.
    - Effective at immobilizing them quickly.
- **Tips for safety:**
    - Apply the submission quickly and with control to avoid prolonged entanglements.

---

### **Control-Based Submissions**

#### 10. **Buggy Choke (From Bottom Side Control)**

- **Why it’s safe:**
    - Counters an opponent trying to punch you from side control.
    - Uses your body to trap their head and arm.
- **Tips for safety:**
    - Lock in the choke tightly and quickly transition to escape if necessary.

#### 11. **Rear Triangle Choke (From Back Control)**

- **Why it’s safe:**
    - Combines the benefits of rear control with a tight choke.
    - Opponent cannot punch or kick effectively while trapped.
- **Application:**
    - Secure back control, isolate one arm, and lock your legs in a triangle configuration.

---

### **Key Points to Avoid Dangerous Submissions:**

- **Avoid Submissions That Expose You to Strikes:**
    
    - Don’t attempt **armbars** or **triangles** from closed guard unless you’re confident in your ability to control their posture.
- **Avoid Prolonged Leg Entanglements:**
    
    - Leg locks (e.g., heel hooks) can be risky if the opponent is still mobile enough to kick.
- **Avoid Pulling Guard or Sacrificing Position:**
    
    - Submissions like guillotines or armbars should be applied without pulling guard, as this may result in slams or strikes.
- **Prioritize Control Before Submission:**
    
    - Always secure control of the opponent’s posture, arms, and head before going for a submission.

---

These submissions maximize safety and minimize risk in self-defense situations by controlling the opponent and limiting their ability to strike effectively.
