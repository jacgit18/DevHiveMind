Here’s the **prioritized ranking (1-5)** of the best landmine exercises for **swimming, BJJ, and Muay Thai**, based on **sport-specific carryover, functional strength, and injury prevention**.

### **Tier 1 (Essential – Highest Carryover)**  
**1. Landmine Rotational Press**  
- **Why?** Rotational power is critical for **Muay Thai (round kicks, hooks), BJJ (hip escapes, sweeps), and swimming (freestyle rotation).**  
  
**2. Landmine Anti-Rotation Press**  
- **Why?** Core stability is vital for **BJJ (defensive framing), Muay Thai (balance in kicks), and swimming (streamlined body position).**  
  
**3. Landmine RDL (Single or Double-Arm)**  
- **Why?** Strengthens the posterior chain for **BJJ (takedowns, guard retention), Muay Thai (power in kicks), and swimming (dolphin kicks, starts/turns).**  
  
---  
  
### **Tier 2 (Highly Beneficial – Strong Secondary Movements)**  
**4. Landmine Squat to Press**  
- **Why?** Combines leg drive (Muay Thai clinch, BJJ stand-ups) with overhead strength (swimming strokes).  
  
**5. Landmine Lateral Rotations**  
- **Why?** Reinforces rotational endurance for **Muay Thai (combos), BJJ (guard passing), and swimming (stroke rhythm).**  
  
**6. Landmine T-Bar Row**  
- **Why?** Upper back strength for **BJJ (posture control), swimming (pull phase), and Muay Thai (clinch control).**  
  
---  
  
### **Tier 3 (Useful – Sport-Specific Applications)**  
**7. Landmine Single-Arm Shoulder Press**  
- **Why?** Unilateral strength helps **swimming (stroke balance) and Muay Thai (jab/clinch stability).**  
  
**8. Landmine Reverse Lunge + Rotation**  
- **Why?** Mobility and rotational control benefit **BJJ (hip movement) and Muay Thai (angle changes).**  
  
---  
  
### **Tier 4 (Situational – Good but Limited Impact)**  
**9. Landmine Hip Throw**  
- **Why?** Useful for **BJJ (takedown mechanics) but less transfer to swimming/Muay Thai.**  
  
**10. Landmine Defensive Frame Hold**
