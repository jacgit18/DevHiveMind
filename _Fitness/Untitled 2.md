


For a **sprint-specific warm-up**, the goal is to **activate the nervous system, mobilize joints, increase body temperature**, and **prime movement patterns**. Your list is comprehensive and covers mobility, core, dynamic stretches, and sprint drills. To make it effective and not overly long, it’s best to **structure it into phases**, cap the time (~12–20 min), and use a mix of **reps, duration, and flow** based on the type of exercise.

---

### 🔧 **Suggested Structure (15–20 min total):**

#### **1. Mobility Activation (5–6 mins total)**

Focus: **Controlled, slower movements for range of motion and joint prep**  
**Use Duration: ~30 sec per side or 5–8 reps per side**

- Scorpion Stretch – 30 sec each side
    
- Supine T-position leg raise – 6 reps/side
    
- Internal/External Ankle Circles – 10 reps each direction
    
- Modified Cossack Stretch with Side Reach – 5 reps/side
    
- Lunge with Overhead Lean and Torso Rotation – 5 reps/side
    
- Low Lunge Spiral Fold – 5 reps/side
    
- Spinal Twists – 30 sec
    

✅ _Keep it flowing — think of this as a mobility flow circuit._

---

#### **2. Dynamic Muscle Activation (4–5 mins)**

Focus: **Glutes, core, shoulders, hip flexors**  
**Use Reps: 8–10 reps per side**

- Side-Lying Leg Lifts (or standing version) – 10 reps/side
    
- Single-Leg RDL w/ Knee Drive Swing – 6–8 reps/side
    
- Rotational Side Plank – 3–4 slow rotations per side
    
- Elbow Push-ups (Pike Push-up Focus) – 6–8 reps
    

✅ _These should be slow and controlled to wake up the right muscles._

---

#### **3. Dynamic Movement Prep (5–6 mins)**

Focus: **Running mechanics, rhythm, coordination**  
**Use Duration: ~20–30 sec or 2x 10-meter reps per movement**

- Free Flow Skipping – 20 sec
    
- Backward Walking & Skipping – 10 meters each
    
- Carioca – 2 x 10 meters
    
- Side Shuffles – 2 x 10 meters
    
- Lateral Leg Swings – 10/side
    
- Linear Leg Swings – 10/side
    
- High Knee Circles – 10/leg
    
- Walking Lunges with Reach – 6 reps/leg
    

✅ _This should flow like a “movement parade.” You can do this moving forward in space or on the spot._

---

### 🟢 **Finisher – Sprint Prep (1 min)**

If you’re leading into actual sprinting:

- Gradual sprint build-up over 10–20 meters x 2
    
- Emphasize tall posture, arm drive, and cadence
    

---

### 1. **Modified Cossack Stretch with Side Reach**

- **Original**: "Cosack position but on knee with extended leg, extend V arms leaning to alternating side"
    
- **Refined Name**: _Half-Kneeling Cossack Reach_
    
- **Description**: Start in a kneeling position with one leg extended out to the side (similar to a Cossack squat, but down on the knee). Arms are raised overhead in a V shape. Lean your upper body toward the extended leg, alternating sides to feel a stretch through the side body and adductors.
    

---

### 2. **Lunge with Overhead Lean and Torso Rotation**

- **Original**: "Lung position extend V arms leaning back rotating towards one shoulder"
    
- **Refined Name**: _High Lunge Overhead Backbend with Rotation_
    
- **Description**: In a high lunge position, extend both arms overhead in a V shape. Lean back slightly while rotating the torso toward the front leg’s side. This opens up the hip flexors, spine, and obliques.
    

---

### 3. **Low Lunge Rotation and Side Bend Over Front Leg**

- **Original**: "Lunge position rotate and side bend down over the front leg that is kneeling for the lunge almost trying to look at extended leg"
    
- **Refined Name**: _Low Lunge Spiral Fold_
    
- **Description**: From a low lunge (back knee down), rotate your torso and side bend over the front leg, almost like you're trying to look toward the back (extended) leg. This movement deeply mobilizes the spine and hips while stretching the hamstrings and side body.

## 🔥 **RAISE (Increase Body Temp & Heart Rate)**

> These are **dynamic** movements that elevate heart rate — not commonly done on your side, but here's what applies:

### **Dynamic (On Side)**

- **Side-to-Side Rolling** – Roll from side to side across a mat, using arms and hips to initiate; great for warm blood flow in spine/core.
    
- **Side-Lying Bicycles** – Pedal your top leg like a bike in the air to raise HR slightly and warm up hips.
    

---

## 💡 **ACTIVATE (Engage Muscle Groups)**

> Controlled, repetitive contractions to wake up stabilizers and movers (often glutes, core, shoulders).

### **Dynamic**

- **Side-Lying Leg Lifts** – Top leg lifts straight up and down; targets glute medius.
    
- **Clamshells** – Knees bent, feet together, open top knee like a clamshell.
    
- **Side-Lying Hip Circles** – Lift top leg and draw slow circles in the air.
    
- **Side Plank Leg Raises** – From a side plank, lift top leg up/down; glutes + core activation.
    

### **Static**

- **Side Plank Hold** – Elbow under shoulder, hips lifted; hold 15–30 sec per side.
    
- **Side-Lying Isometric Clam Hold** – Open clamshell and hold at top range.
    

---

## 💥 **POTENTIATE (Prep for Explosive Work)**

> Primer exercises—often isometric or resisted—to stimulate CNS and movement patterns used in training.

### **Dynamic**

- **Side Plank with Knee Drive** – From a side plank, explosively drive the top knee toward the chest, mimicking sprint mechanics.
    
- **Band-Resisted Side Clamshells** – Add a loop band for more neural drive; short bursts of powerful reps.



**Barbell Exercises: Upper vs Lower Body Considerations**

Upper body barbell exercises—like the bench press—can sometimes place unnecessary strain on the wrists due to the fixed, straight bar grip. If the grip doesn’t align naturally with your wrist and shoulder joints, it can cause discomfort or even injury over time.

In contrast, barbell movements for the lower body (like squats and deadlifts) or full-body lifts often allow for a more natural grip or distribute load in a way that’s generally better tolerated.

🔍 **Key Takeaway:**  
Pay attention to how your body feels. If a movement feels off—especially in your wrists, shoulders, or joints—it may be worth adjusting your grip, switching to dumbbells, or using specialty bars (like a Swiss bar or safety squat bar) that allow for a more neutral position.
