# Energy Costs of Defensive Options in Striking

In striking, different defensive options have varying energy costs based on movement, muscle engagement, and reaction time. Here’s a breakdown from least to most energy-intensive:

## 🔹 Lower Energy Cost (More Efficient)

1. **Blocking** – Uses minimal movement, mainly arm positioning. It’s efficient but can cause damage over time.  
2. **Parrying** – A slight redirection of strikes, requiring small, precise movements.  
3. **Hand Fighting** – Intercepting and controlling an opponent’s hands takes some energy but is efficient if used strategically.  

## 🔸 Moderate Energy Cost  

4. **Head Movement** – Slipping and rolling require core engagement and coordination. If overused or exaggerated, it can become tiring.  
5. **Footwork (Small Adjustments)** – Subtle angle changes or pivots are efficient but require awareness and balance.  

## 🔺 Higher Energy Cost (More Draining)  

6. **Larger Footwork Movements** – Big steps, circling, or retreating require leg endurance and can fatigue you quickly.  
7. **Constant Hand Fighting** – If done excessively, it becomes a battle of grip strength and energy expenditure.  
8. **Explosive Defensive Maneuvers** – Jumping back, ducking too low, or rapid lateral movements burn energy fast.  

## ⚡ Key Takeaway  

- **Blocking & parrying** are the most energy-efficient.  
- **Head movement & small footwork adjustments** are moderately taxing but effective.  
- **Big footwork movements & constant hand fighting** drain the most energy over time.  

The best approach is **layering** these defenses—mixing efficient options (blocking, parrying) with evasive techniques (head movement, footwork) only when needed to conserve energy.
