Switching up your defensive tactics depends on the opponent’s approach, your positioning, and your energy level. Here’s when you might want to transition between different defensive options:

### **1. Hand Fighting → Parrying**

- **When to switch:** If your opponent is disengaging from hand fighting and throwing clean, straight punches.
    
- **Why:** Parrying is more efficient when dealing with straight punches rather than staying in a clinch-like hand-fighting exchange.
    

### **2. Parrying → Head Movement**

- **When to switch:** If your opponent is feinting a lot or mixing in hooks after straight shots.
    
- **Why:** Overcommitting to parries against feints can leave you open. Head movement is a safer response when punches come from angles.
    

### **3. Head Movement → Footwork**

- **When to switch:** If you're against the ropes or backed into a corner.
    
- **Why:** Slipping alone won’t get you out of a bad position; you need to use footwork to reset.
    

### **4. Footwork → Blocking**

- **When to switch:** If your opponent is cutting you off and you can’t escape laterally.
    
- **Why:** If moving isn’t an option, tightening up your guard and blocking can buy you time to find an opening.
    

### **5. Blocking → Hand Fighting**

- **When to switch:** If you're absorbing too many shots on the guard and need to disrupt your opponent’s rhythm.
    
- **Why:** Hand fighting can smother their offense and give you a chance to regain control.
    

### **6. Hand Fighting → Footwork**

- **When to switch:** If your opponent is physically stronger and outmuscling you in the clinch or hand-fighting exchanges.
    
- **Why:** Instead of engaging in a strength battle, footwork allows you to create space and reset.
    

Each defensive option has strengths and weaknesses, so knowing when to switch between them keeps you unpredictable and harder to hit.
