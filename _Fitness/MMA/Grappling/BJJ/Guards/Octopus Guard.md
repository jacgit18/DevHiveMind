Octopus Guard is a unique and unconventional position in Jiu-Jitsu that typically involves using an overhook or underhook while wrapping your arm around your opponent’s back, often from a seated or half-guard position. Here’s a list of ways to transition into Octopus Guard from different positions:

---

### **From Half Guard**

1. **Basic Underhook Entry**
    
    - Secure an underhook on the far side and start sliding your arm deep around their back.
    - Shrimp your hips out and rotate your torso to create the angle for Octopus Guard.
2. **Reverse Shrimp Entry**
    
    - From a flattened position, bridge to create space.
    - Use the space to rotate your torso and reach around their back for the Octopus grip.
3. **Opponent Overcommitting to Pressure**
    
    - When your opponent leans heavily forward in half guard, slip your arm around their back and lock in Octopus Guard.

---

### **From Closed Guard**

4. **Overhook Setup**
    
    - Use an overhook on one arm to break their posture.
    - Shrimp your hips out to the overhook side, reach around their back, and establish Octopus Guard.
5. **Hip Bump Fake**
    
    - Fake a hip bump sweep to get them to react.
    - Use the opening to reach around their back and lock in Octopus Guard.
6. **Collar Grip Entry**
    
    - Grab their collar with one hand to pull them forward.
    - Release the grip and transition your arm around their back to establish Octopus Guard.

---

### **From Butterfly Guard**

7. **Arm Drag to Octopus Guard**
    
    - Use an arm drag to pull their weight forward.
    - Wrap your arm around their back and establish Octopus Guard before they recover posture.
8. **Overhook Entry**
    
    - Secure an overhook on one arm, kick your leg out, and rotate your body to get the angle.
    - Transition into Octopus Guard by sliding your arm deep around their back.

---

### **From Seated Guard**

9. **Opponent Reaching Over**
    
    - If they reach forward to grab or pressure, dive your arm around their back while scooting in.
    - Pull yourself into Octopus Guard.
10. **Transition from Arm Drag**
    
    - Perform an arm drag, but instead of going for their back, slip your arm around their far side and lock in Octopus Guard.

---

### **From Side Control (Bottom)**

11. **Opponent Overcommitting Weight**
    - If they lean heavily forward, bridge and shrimp to create space.
    - Slide your arm under their far armpit and around their back to establish Octopus Guard.

---

### **From Standing or Takedown Scrambles**

12. **Single Leg Counter**
    
    - If they attempt a single-leg takedown, sprawl and use the space to wrap your arm around their back as you sit to guard.
13. **Collar Tie Transition**
    
    - If you have a collar tie in a standing position, use it to break their posture forward.
    - Drop to guard while slipping your arm around their back.

---

### **From Turtle (Top or Bottom)**

14. **Opponent Reaching Up**
    
    - From bottom turtle, if your opponent reaches around your torso, slip your arm under theirs and around their back to transition to Octopus Guard.
15. **Front Headlock Counter**
    
    - If they grab a front headlock while you’re in turtle, rotate your hips and reach around their back to escape into Octopus Guard.

---

### **Drills to Practice Getting to Octopus Guard**

- Practice transitioning to Octopus Guard from various open guard setups (butterfly, seated, or half).
- Drill overhook and underhook entries to develop muscle memory for reaching around their back.
- Work on combining Octopus Guard setups with sweeps or submissions to make the transitions seamless.

By using these entries and transitions, you can add Octopus Guard to your arsenal and create opportunities to sweep, take the back, or transition into submissions.
