Modified Open Guard (Feet on Hips or Biceps)

- **Why it's effective:**
    - Maintains distance and neutralizes their ability to punch effectively.
    - Controls their posture by using your legs and grips on their wrists.
- **Key strategies:**
    - Push against their hips with your feet to maintain distance.
    - If they throw punches, use your feet to push off and create space to stand up.
    - Transition to sweeps (e.g., push sweep) if they try to close distance.





**Using the Wall to Control and Limit Movement in Full Guard**

When grappling, particularly in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ) or MMA, utilizing the environment—like the wall or cage—can be a strategic advantage. Here's an expanded breakdown of how to move your opponent and use the wall to control them while in their full guard:

### **Goal**

The objective is to restrict your opponent’s ability to maneuver and execute submissions, sweeps, or escapes by reducing their mobility and options using the wall or cage as a physical barrier.

### **Steps to Achieve This**

1. **Create Pressure and Positioning**
    
    - **Posture Up**: Maintain a strong, upright posture to avoid being broken down by their grips or attempts to control your head or arms.
    - **Engage Your Hips**: Keep your hips low and apply pressure through your opponent’s midsection to prevent them from freely moving or angling for attacks.
2. **Control Their Hips**
    
    - **Hand Placement**: Place one or both hands on their hips or torso to pin them down and prevent them from shrimping or creating angles.
    - **Knee Pressure**: Use your knees to pinch their hips or thighs, keeping them flat on their back.
3. **Move Them Toward the Wall**
    
    - **Shift Their Position**: Use small, deliberate movements to drag their back or shoulders toward the wall.
        - If you're in MMA, use shoulder pressure and short, controlled strikes to disrupt their rhythm and inch them backward.
        - In BJJ, you can grip their gi or use underhooks to maneuver them.
4. **Trap Them Against the Wall**
    
    - **Angle Their Spine**: Once their upper back or shoulders are against the wall, position yourself so that their hips are flat and their spine is angled awkwardly. This eliminates their ability to turn or pivot effectively.
    - **Head and Shoulder Control**: Use your hand or forearm to pin their head or shoulders against the wall for further control.
5. **Maintain Dominance**
    
    - **Limit Their Guard Options**: Once they’re against the wall, their ability to open their guard or attack with submissions is reduced. From here:
        - Keep your elbows tight to avoid armbars.
        - Monitor for attempts to create space with their legs or use the wall to climb.
6. **Advance Your Position**
    
    - **Break Open the Guard**: Use pressure against the wall to open their legs, making it harder for them to re-close their guard.
    - **Pass the Guard**: Once their guard is open, move to half guard, side control, or a dominant top position.

### **Why the Wall Works**

- **Mobility is Reduced**: The wall acts as a barrier, preventing them from using space to create angles or generate momentum for attacks.
- **Energy Drain**: Your opponent must exert more effort to move or defend, which can fatigue them over time.
- **Psychological Pressure**: Being pressed against the wall often forces opponents into defensive mode, giving you the mental and physical advantage.

### **Things to Watch Out For**

- **Wall Walks**: Opponents with experience may use the wall to climb back to their feet. Be prepared to control their posture or pin their legs to prevent this.
- **Submissions**: Even when against the wall, your opponent may attempt submissions like guillotines or kimuras. Keep your posture strong and defend appropriately.
- **Illegal Techniques (MMA Rules)**: If in MMA, ensure you’re not using techniques like grabbing the cage, which could result in penalties.
