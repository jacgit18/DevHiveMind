When dealing with a passive bigger opponent in **turtle position**, your strategy changes dramatically based on whether you're **on top** (attacking) or **on bottom** (defending/scrambling). Here’s how to leverage your speed and agility in both scenarios:

  

---

  

### **If YOU’RE ON BOTTOM (Turtled)**

*(Your goal: Escape without getting smashed or giving up back control)*  

**Bigger opponents will try to flatten you or crush you with weight. Counter with movement and frames:**

  

1. **DON’T STAY STATIC**  

   - Immediately work to **re-guard** or **explode into a scramble**. Never let them settle their weight.  

   - **Key moves:** Sit-out, Granby roll, or technical stand-up.  

  

2. **FRAME AGGRESSIVELY**  

   - **Forearm frames** against their hips/thighs to create space.  

   - **T-Rex arms** (elbows tight to ribs) to avoid them locking a seatbelt.  

  

3. **ATTACK THEIR BASE**  

   - If they lean forward: **Sit-out** → spin to front headlock or take the back.  

   - If they post a hand: **Knee-push escape** → shove their posted knee to collapse them.  

  

4. **GRANBY ROLL (Lifesaver for small players)**  

   - Tuck your head, roll over your shoulder, and recover guard or scramble to top position.  

   - *Perfect for when they try to drive weight forward*.  

  

5. **IF STUCK: Half-Guard > Side Control**  

   - If they start passing, accept **half-guard** (knee shield) instead of flat side control.  

   - Use frames to recompose full guard or sweep.  

  

---

  

### **If YOU’RE ON TOP (Attacking Turtle)**  

*(Your goal: Break their base, expose their back, or force a turnover)*  

**Bigger players will use strength to post and resist. Use angles and speed:**

  

1. **NEVER CHEST-TO-BACK**  

   - Avoid draping over them — they’ll bench-press you off or stand up.  

   - **Stay on your feet/toes**, circle laterally, and attack from angles.  

  

2. **BREAK THEIR BASE**  

   - **Hook their far knee** (inside hook): Pull it toward you to collapse their posture.  

   - **Knee-pick the posted leg**: Kick their calf/knee out if they post a leg wide.  

  

3. **SPIN TO THE BACK / SIDE**  

   - **Rolling back take**: If they defend one side, cartwheel over their head to the other side.  

   - **Corkscrew pass**: Drive your shoulder into their ribs while circling → force them to flat side control.  

  

4. **ATTACK LIMBS**  

   - **Far-side arm drag**: Pull their posted arm across their body → take back or mount.  

   - **Near-side wrist control**: Trap their arm and step over for an armbar or kimura trap.  

  

5. **LEG LOCKS (If legal)**  

   - **Rolling toehold**: If they post a leg, grab the ankle and roll.  

   - **Heel hook entries**: From headquarters position (one leg controlled).  

  

6. **SPIRAL RIDE (Best for stalling big players)**  

   - Wrap their waist, hook their far thigh with your leg, and drive them sideways onto their hip → exposes back or sets up north-south.  

  

---

  

### **Key Principles for Both Positions**

- **Speed > Strength**: Chain attacks rapidly. If one fails, immediately transition (e.g., arm drag fails → switch to knee pick).  

- **Deny Posts**: Knock away their hand/knee posts the moment they touch the mat.  

- **Exhaust Them**: Keep them reacting. Bigger athletes gas faster when constantly defending.  

- **Safety**: When on bottom, protect your neck. When on top, stay balanced to avoid being rolled.  

  

---

  

### **Example Sequences**

- **On Top**:  

  *Strip their post → spiral ride → they post a hand → trap the arm → rolling back take.*  

- **On Bottom**:  

  *Frame on their hip → sit-out → they sprawl → Granby roll → recover guard.*  

  

**Small Fighter Mindset:** "If I’m not moving, I’m losing." Force errors by making them defend 2-3 actions per second. Against size, your gas tank and agility are weapons — use them! 🥋
