Here's a list of sweeps specifically from the **Mount**, **S-Mount**, and **Turtle Guard** positions:

---

### **Sweeps from Bottom Mount**

1. **Bridge and Roll Sweep (Uppercut Sweep)**:
    
    - **How**: From bottom mount, you bridge your hips hard to one side, creating space, and roll your opponent over, landing on top. It's a classic reversal when your opponent has low pressure or is controlling with an underhook.
2. **Elbow Escape to Sweep**:
    
    - **How**: You use the elbow escape to create space, turning your body and framing against your opponent. From there, you can either recover guard or sweep them back into side control.
3. **Trap and Roll Sweep**:
    
    - **How**: From mount, trap one of your opponent’s arms (such as with a cross-face grip), and then perform a bridge and roll, sweeping them over to one side, landing in their guard or side control.
4. **Arm Trap and Sweep**:
    
    - **How**: When your opponent is postured up, you can trap one of their arms (usually the arm farthest from you), then use your legs to elevate their torso and sweep them over.

---

### **Sweeps from Bottom S-Mount**

1. **Trap the Far Arm Sweep**:
    
    - **How**: In S-mount, your opponent will likely be trying to base out. If you can trap their far arm with your legs or arms, you can use it to push and off-balance them, then sweep them over with your hips.
2. **Back Take from S-Mount**:
    
    - **How**: If your opponent’s weight is shifted too far forward or backward, you can use a back take, pulling them into a sweep and securing back control. This can be viewed as a sweep into a more dominant position.
3. **Armbar Sweep**:
    
    - **How**: When you have control of your opponent's arm in S-mount, you can transition into an armbar or sweep them over while using the pressure from your hips to force them into a more vulnerable position.
4. **Knee to Belly Sweep**:
    
    - **How**: From the S-mount position, when your opponent starts to defend by framing or trying to escape, you can use a knee-to-belly motion to transition into a sweep that lands you in side control or knee-on-belly.

---

### **Sweeps from Bottom Turtle Guard**

1. **Granby Roll**:
    
    - **How**: From the turtle position, you can perform a Granby Roll, where you roll over your shoulder and head to escape the turtle, which may lead to a sweep or reversal if your opponent's weight is off balance.
2. **Sit-Out to Sweep**:
    
    - **How**: A sit-out from turtle involves sitting to one side and rotating your hips to face your opponent, allowing you to sweep them or recover a better position like side control or even back control.
3. **Knee Tap from Turtle**:
    
    - **How**: When your opponent attempts to press down on you from the turtle, you can use a knee-tap sweep by reaching across their body and grabbing their knee, pulling them down into a sweep and onto their back.
4. **Single Leg Sweep**:
    
    - **How**: In turtle guard, if your opponent is applying pressure or attempting to control you, you can reach for their leg, elevate it, and sweep them over to the side, ending up in a dominant position like side control.
5. **Hip Heist Sweep**:
    
    - **How**: From turtle, when your opponent tries to control your upper body, you can pop your hips to one side and roll into them, reversing the position and sweeping them onto their back.

---

Each of these sweeps focuses on using leverage, body positioning, and momentum to transition from bottom mount, S-mount, or turtle guard into a more dominant position or reversal.
