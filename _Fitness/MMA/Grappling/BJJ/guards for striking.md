In Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ), hand fighting and circling inside or outside the arms depends on the goal of the position. Unlike striking, where circling from the outside is generally safer to avoid getting hit, BJJ often allows for more flexibility in circling from either inside or outside based on the control and leverage you’re trying to achieve.  
  
Circling Inside the Arm in BJJ:  
  
Advantages: Circling inside the arm can be effective for gaining inside control, which is crucial for setting up grips, frames, underhooks, or dominant positions. For example:  
  
Underhooks: Circling inside allows you to dig underhooks, which are powerful tools for controlling your opponent in positions like half guard or standing clinch.  
  
Collar Ties: From inside, you can establish strong grips or set up sweeps and submissions.  
  
Submission Setups: Circling inside can create openings for techniques like armbars, triangle chokes, or controlling a limb in the gi.  
  
  
Risks: Circling inside may expose you to overhooks, guillotine attempts, or wrist control, so you need to combine it with proper head position and body alignment to avoid being countered.  
  
  
Circling Outside the Arm in BJJ:  
  
Advantages: Circling outside is useful when you’re looking to clear grips or disengage while staying safe. It allows you to maintain distance and avoid being tied up or countered. For example:  
  
Grip Fighting: Circling outside is effective for breaking your opponent’s grips or setting up your own while avoiding direct confrontation.  
  
Passing Guard: Circling outside can help you avoid getting caught in frames or entangled during guard passing.  
  
  
Similarities to Striking: Just like in striking, circling from the outside keeps you out of range of direct attacks (e.g., wrist locks, armlocks, or aggressive collar ties).  
  
  
Key Takeaway:  
  
In BJJ, you can circle either inside or outside depending on your goal and the situation. The primary difference from striking is that inside control is often a desired position in BJJ, while in striking it can be riskier due to proximity to attacks. Your choice should be based on the control you want to establish and your ability to defend against counters.
