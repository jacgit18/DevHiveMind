### **Common Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ) Sweeps Categorized by Guard Positions**

This list focuses on high-percentage sweeps in both gi and no-gi contexts, emphasizing practicality, particularly in scenarios where striking may occur.

---

### **From Closed Guard**

1. **Scissor Sweep**  
    Off-balance an opponent by scissoring their base with your legs while pulling their arm and collar.
    
    - **Why it's effective:**
        - Simple and works well when the opponent is postured for striking.
        - Keeps you protected while off-balancing them.
    - **How to do it:**
        - Open your guard, placing one shin across their torso and your other leg on the mat.
        - Grip their collar or wrist and pull forward while kicking with your top leg and sweeping their base leg.
2. **Hip Bump Sweep**  
    Exploit an upright posture by sitting up, trapping an arm, and driving your hips to topple them.
    
    - **Why it's effective:**
        - Generates powerful momentum and transitions to mount.
    - **How to do it:**
        - Sit up from guard, controlling one arm and posting your opposite hand.
        - Drive your hips upward and roll them over.

3. **Flower Sweep**  
    Variation of the pendulum sweep, using a grip on their pant leg or sleeve.
    
    - **Why it's effective:**
        - Combines control with momentum, neutralizing strikes.
    - **How to do it:**
        - Underhook their leg and pull their arm across.
        - Swing your free leg in a pendulum motion to sweep and take mount.
4. **Arm Drag to Back Take Sweep**  
    Drag their arm to off-balance them, leading to a sweep or back take.
    

Double Ankle Sweep
- **Why it's effective:**
    - Exploits opponents who stand in your guard to rain down strikes.
    - Can quickly neutralize their attacks and put you on top.
- **How to do it:**
    - If they stand in your closed guard, control both ankles with your hands.
    - Push your hips up while pulling their ankles to sweep them.
    - Follow into mount or side control.

### **From Open Guard**

1. **Tripod Sweep**  
    Use one foot on their hip and another behind their ankle to trip them backward.
    
    - **Why it's effective:**
        - Exploits standing or postured opponents while maintaining distance.
    - **How to do it:**
        - Control their ankle and wrist, push with your foot on their hip, and pull with your grips to sweep.
2. **Sickle Sweep**  
    Hook their leg and control their arm or collar to sweep sideways.
    
3. **De La Riva Sweep**  
    Hook their leg and use grips to off-balance and sweep (_risky in striking_).
    
4. **X-Guard Sweep**  
    Elevate their leg with your hooks and tilt them to sweep (_risky in striking_).

Balloon Sweep (Tomoe Nage)
- **Why it's effective:**
    - Uses your legs to throw the opponent over your head, keeping you safe from strikes.
    - Can lead to standing up or transitioning to a dominant position.
- **How to do it:**
    - From guard, grab their wrist and collar, and place a foot on their hip.
    - Pull them forward and use your foot to lift and flip them over you.
    - Follow up with a scramble to a better position.

Push Sweep (Hip Elevation Sweep)
- **Why it's effective:**
    - Uses your legs to create space and take advantage of their forward pressure.
    - Keeps their punches or elbows from reaching you.
- **How to do it:**
    - From open guard, place one foot on their hip and the other foot behind their knee.
    - Push with your foot on the hip while pulling their upper body forward to off-balance them.

### **From Half Guard**

1. **Old School Sweep**  
    Trap their foot and roll them forward as they apply pressure.
    
    - **Why it's effective:**
        - Neutralizes strikes by entangling their leg and taking top control.
    - **How to do it:**
        - Underhook their far arm and secure their trapped foot.
        - Drive forward and roll them over.
2. **Knee Tap Sweep**  
    Use an underhook to drive forward while controlling their knee to destabilize them.
    
3. **Coyote Sweep**  
    Perform a single-leg motion from half guard to roll and sweep (_risky in striking_).
    
4. **Electric Chair Sweep**  
    Stretch their legs into a split to destabilize them (_risky in striking_).
    

---

### **From Butterfly Guard**

1. **Basic Butterfly Sweep**  
    Use an underhook and elevate with your hooks to sweep.
    
    - **Why it's effective:**
        - Keeps the opponent's posture broken, reducing strike potential.
    - **How to do it:**
        - Underhook one arm and grab their wrist or shoulder.
        - Use your hook to lift their leg and sweep them sideways.
2. **Arm Drag to Back Take**  
    Drag their arm across their body to create sweeping opportunities or take their back.
    
3. **Hook Sweep**  
    Pull their posture forward and tip them over with a hook (_risky in striking_).
    

---

### **From Deep Half Guard**

1. **Homer Simpson Sweep**  
    Roll under their base and secure top position.
    
2. **Plan B Sweep**  
    Transition to a roll-under sweep if the Old School Sweep fails.
    
3. **Waiter Sweep**  
    Elevate their leg, tilt them sideways, and take top control (_risky in striking_).
    

---

### **From Standing (Opponent on Top)**

1. **Lumberjack Sweep**  
    Control both ankles and pull forward to sweep them onto their back.
    
    - **Why it's effective:**
        - Exploits weight distribution and counters standing strikes.
    - **How to do it:**
        - Grab their ankles and lift your hips to off-balance them.
        - Pull their ankles forward and follow into top control.
2. **Sit-Up Guard to Single Leg**  
    Transition from a seated guard into a single-leg takedown.
    

---


### **From Single-Leg X-Guard**

1. **Back Sweep**:  
    Roll backward with control of their leg, sweeping them onto their back.
    
2. **Ankle Pick Sweep** (_risky in striking_):  
    Control their ankle and lift with hooks to tip them backward.
    


### **Key Considerations for Striking Scenarios**

1. **Control Posture First**  
    Focus on sweeps that break posture (e.g., butterfly or hip bump sweeps).
    
2. **Manage Distance**  
    Stay either very close to smother strikes or far enough to avoid their reach. Avoid mid-range positions.
    
3. **Prioritize Dominant Positions**  
    Always aim to sweep into mount, side control, or a standing escape.
    
4. **Use Timing and Leverage:** Wait for them to overcommit to a strike or movement and exploit their balance.
5. **Transition Quickly:** Don’t hang out on your back; sweep to get on top or create space to stand up.
6. **Protect Your Head:** Always use frames or grips to shield yourself from strikes during the sweep.


---

### **Sweeps to Avoid in Striking Scenarios**

- **Inverted Guard Sweeps**: Expose your head to strikes.
- **Spider/Lasso Guard Sweeps**: Rely on grips impractical in no-gi or self-defense.

---

### **Best Sweeps for Real-World Scenarios**

- **Hip Bump Sweep**
- **Butterfly Sweep**
- **Technical Stand-Up Sweep**

These sweeps prioritize safety, posture control, and transitions to dominant positions.
