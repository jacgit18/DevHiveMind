Here’s a categorized list of BJJ submissions broken down by bottom and top positions, along with how they are applied depending on your position relative to your opponent.  
  
  
---  
  
Bottom Position Submissions  
  
Full Guard (Closed Guard)  
  
Armbar: Use posture breaks and arm isolation.  
  
Triangle Choke: Trap one arm and one side of the neck, closing your legs around their head.  
  
Kimura: Wrist control and overhook lead to a shoulder lock.  
  
Guillotine Choke: Attack when the opponent leans forward or exposes their neck.  
  
Cross Collar Choke: Deep grips on the lapel with pressure.  
  
Omoplata: Attack an arm, transitioning to a shoulder lock.  
  
  
Open Guards  
  
Spider Guard:  
  
Triangle Choke: Control arms to isolate the head and one arm.  
  
Armbars: Manipulate grips to transition to submissions.  
  
  
De La Riva Guard:  
  
Triangle Choke: Use off-balancing to secure the submission.  
  
Leg Locks: Access kneebars or straight ankle locks during sweeps.  
  
  
Butterfly Guard:  
  
Guillotine: Effective against pressure passers.  
  
Armbar: Create openings during sweeps.  
  
  
X-Guard:  
  
Straight Ankle Locks: Leg entanglements.  
  
Heel Hooks: Focus on the opponent’s legs during transitions.  
  
  
  
Half Guard (Including Variations)  
  
Kimura: Control the arm while locking down the opponent’s posture.  
  
Guillotine Choke: Attack when the opponent tries to pressure forward.  
  
Leg Locks: Kneebars and heel hooks from entanglements.  
  
Deep Half Guard:  
  
Back Takes to RNC: Create sweeps and take the back for submissions.  
  
  
  
  
---  
  
Top Position Submissions  
  
Passing Full Guard  
  
Arm Triangle: Control head and arm after breaking through the guard.  
  
Americana: Target a pinned arm once you pass into a dominant position.  
  
Kimura: Transition from guard passing to side control.  
  
  
Side Control  
  
Americana: Trap the arm and crank towards the head.  
  
Kimura: Use wrist control to isolate the shoulder.  
  
Arm Triangle: Transition by trapping the opponent’s head and arm.  
  
Baseball Bat Choke: Apply lapel grips for choke pressure.  
  
  
Mount  
  
Armbar: Isolate the arm from mount to swing into the lock.  
  
Triangle from Mount: Transition by trapping their arm and wrapping legs around.  
  
Ezekiel Choke: Use sleeves to apply a forearm choke.  
  
Cross Collar Choke: Deep lapel grips apply choke pressure.  
  
  
North-South  
  
Kimura: Leverage arm isolation.  
  
North-South Choke: Use head-and-arm control to apply choke pressure.  
  
  
Back Mount  
  
Rear Naked Choke (RNC): Use seatbelt grips and take their back.  
  
Bow and Arrow Choke: Attack with lapel grips while controlling their leg.  
  
  
Leg Entanglements (Top/Passing Guards)  
  
Straight Ankle Locks: Attack exposed legs.  
  
Heel Hooks: Target rotational pressure on the knee.  
  
Kneebars: Extend and isolate the opponent’s leg.  
  
  
  
---  
  
Summary  
  
Bottom Position: Emphasis on sweeps transitioning into submissions like armbars, triangles, and guillotines. Open guards lead to both defensive setups and offensive attacks.  
  
Top Position: Submissions focus on controlling the opponent after passing their guard, including arm triangles, chokes, and leg locks.  
  
  
This framework highlights how submissions vary based on positional control and leverage principles.



In Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, there are several open guards that work effectively when your opponent remains on their knees or in a low posture. These guards allow you to control, create space, and set up sweeps or submissions without relying on your opponent standing. Here are some options:  
  
  
---  
  
1. Butterfly Guard  
  
Overview: Sit up with your legs bent and feet hooking inside your opponent’s thighs. Your hands control their sleeves, wrists, or collars, and your posture is upright or slightly leaning back.  
  
Why it works: Butterfly guard is great for controlling a kneeling opponent by lifting or sweeping them using your hooks and grips. It's versatile for sweeps and transitions into attacks like armlocks or guillotines.  
  
  
  
---  
  
2. Half Guard  
  
Overview: Trap one of your opponent's legs between yours while controlling their upper body with an underhook, overhook, or collar grip.  
  
Why it works: Half guard is a solid position for creating space, setting up sweeps (like the Old School or Deep Half sweeps), or transitioning into submissions like a kimura or back take.  
  
  
  
---  
  
3. Seated Guard (Sit-Up Guard)  
  
Overview: Sit up with your legs in front, feet ready to push against your opponent’s thighs or hips. Use grips on their sleeves, lapels, or collar to control distance.  
  
Why it works: This is a flexible position that allows you to create distance, break grips, and move into single-leg sweeps or transition into other guards.  
  
  
  
---  
  
4. Cross Collar and Sleeve Guard  
  
Overview: Use a cross-collar grip and control one of your opponent’s sleeves while keeping your legs in an open position. Your feet can go on their hips or thighs.  
  
Why it works: The cross-collar grip provides excellent control over their posture, and the sleeve grip helps you set up arm drags, triangles, or sweeps like the scissor sweep.  
  
  
  
---  
  
5. Closed Guard Transitions to Open  
  
Overview: Start in closed guard and transition into an open position by placing feet on the opponent’s hips or gripping their sleeves while maintaining a solid connection with your legs.  
  
Why it works: This gradual transition allows you to maintain control and create space without needing them to stand up. You can move into spider guard, lasso guard, or collar-and-sleeve guard depending on how they react.  
  
  
  
---  
  
6. Collar Tie and Shin-to-Shin Guard  
  
Overview: Control their posture with one hand gripping the collar or head, and place your shin against their shin. Use this to off-balance or transition into a sweep.  
  
Why it works: This guard disrupts their base and allows you to move into stronger positions like single-leg x-guard without them standing.  
  
  
  
---  
  
7. Z Guard (Knee Shield)  
  
Overview: From a half-guard position, place your top shin across their chest or hips while controlling their upper body with an underhook or frame.  
  
Why it works: The knee shield creates space, making it harder for them to pressure you. It's an excellent platform for sweeps, submissions, or transitioning into other guards.  
  
  
  
---  
  
8. Spider Guard on Knees  
  
Overview: While your opponent is kneeling, use grips on their sleeves and place your feet on their biceps. Adjust pressure with your legs to create space or off-balance them.  
  
Why it works: Spider guard can be adapted for kneeling opponents to control their posture and arms, leading to sweeps or submissions.  
  
  
  
---  
  
9. Deep Half Guard  
  
Overview: Get under your opponent’s hips by scooping beneath one of their legs while trapping their thigh with your legs.  
  
Why it works: Deep half guard is great for sweeping or transitioning into leg entanglements while keeping you safe from pressure passes.  
  
  
  
---  
  
10. Scissor Guard  
  
Overview: Use a cross-collar and sleeve grip, one leg across their midsection, and the other leg placed on the ground. Push and pull to create a sweeping motion.  
  
Why it works: This classic guard works well against a kneeling opponent and allows you to maintain control and set up basic sweeps.  
  
  
  
---  
  
Key Tips:  
  
Control Grips: Always prioritize strong grips on the collar, sleeves, or wrists to control their posture.  
  
Stay Active: Use your legs to constantly push, pull, and off-balance your opponent.  
  
Transitions: Be ready to flow between guards to adapt to your opponent’s reactions.  
  
  
By combining these guards, you’ll have a strong arsenal to control and attack opponents who choose to stay low rather than stand up.
