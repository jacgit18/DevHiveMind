The difference between the **"classic" Judo hip throw (O Goshi)**—where you grip **under the arm (over the back)**—and the **"collar-grip" variation (Koshi Guruma)**—where you grip **the collar/neck**—boils down to **control, leverage, and transitional options**. Here’s how they compare in terms of dynamic versatility:

  

---

  

### **1. O Goshi (Under-Arm Grip)**

- **Grip:** Arm over uke's back, hand on their belt/hip.  

- **Mechanics:** Full hip contact, rotational throw.  

- **Best For:** Pure power throws, beginners (fundamental technique).  

  

#### **Dynamic Options:**  

✅ **Stronger Lifting Power:** Easier to load uke onto your hips for a high-amplitude throw.  

✅ **Safer for Transition to Groundwork (Ne Waza):**  

   - After the throw, your arm is already wrapped around uke’s torso, making it easier to pin (e.g., **Kesa Gatame**).  

✅ **Easier to Chain Into Other Hip Throws:**  

   - Can shift to **Ushiro Goshi** (rear hip throw) if uke defends.  

  

#### **Limitations:**  

❌ **Less Head/Neck Control:** Uke can posture up more easily.  

❌ **Harder to Set Up from Standard Grips:** Requires breaking sleeve/collar grips first.  

  

---

  

### **2. Koshi Guruma (Collar Grip)**

- **Grip:** Lapel/collar grip near uke’s neck, other hand on sleeve/elbow.  

- **Mechanics:** Head blocks uke’s shoulder, hip acts as a wheel ("Guruma" = wheel).  

- **Best For:** Speed, setups from standard grips, and **transitional versatility**.  

  

#### **Dynamic Options:**  

✅ **Better Off-Balancing (Kuzushi):**  

   - Collar grip lets you **snap uke’s head forward/down**, disrupting their posture.  

✅ **More Transitions to Other Throws:**  

   - **Seoi Nage (Shoulder Throw):** Drop to morote/seoi if uke resists.  

   - **Tai Otoshi (Body Drop):** If uke stiffens their legs.  

   - **Uchi Mata (Inner Thigh):** Switch to a lifting leg attack.  

✅ **Stronger Standing Submissions:**  

   - Collar grip sets up **chokes (Okuri Eri Jime)** or **arm locks** mid-movement.  

  

#### **Limitations:**  

❌ **Less Pure Lifting Power:** Relies more on off-balancing than brute strength.  

❌ **Risk of Neck Strain:** If uke sprawls or defends hard.  

  

---

  

### **Which Is More Dynamic?**  

**Koshi Guruma (collar grip) wins for transitional variety**, especially in **Judo and BJJ**, because:  

1. **Works from Standard Grips:** No need to fight for underhooks.  

2. **Head Control = More Kuzushi Options:** Easier to chain into other techniques.  

3. **Closer to Submissions:** Collar grips feed directly into strangles.  

  

**O Goshi is stronger for pure throws and groundwork transitions** but requires more grip fighting.  

  

---

  

### **Hybrid Approach: Best of Both Worlds**  

1. **Start with Koshi Guruma** (collar grip) to off-balance.  

2. **If uke resists**, switch to O Goshi (slide arm under) or Seoi Nage.  

3. **Finish with a choke** (e.g., rolling right into a collar choke).  

  

**Pro Tip:** In BJJ, Koshi Guruma’s collar grip is **gold**—it sets up loop chokes or arm drags if the throw fails.
