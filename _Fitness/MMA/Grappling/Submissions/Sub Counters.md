**Reverse a Kimura with an Armbar**: When someone locks a Kimura, transition to an Armbar by isolating their arm.

 **X-Choke to Triangle**: Use one submission to bait and flow into another.

1. **Armbar (Juji Gatame)**  
- **Counter Submission:**  
- **Leg Lock**: As the opponent extends for the armbar, you can use your legs to isolate one of their legs for a counter leg lock.  
- **Triangle Choke**: If you manage to pull your arm out and they remain in a compromised position.  
  
2. **Triangle Choke (Sankaku Jime)**  
- **Counter Submission:**  
- **Ankle Lock**: If you can posture up and isolate their legs.  
- **Kneebar**: By transitioning your hips under their legs as you posture up.  
  
3. **Omoplata**  
- **Counter Submission:**  
- **Straight Ankle Lock**: As they attempt the omoplata, you can attack their leg.  
- **Toe Hold**: If you can isolate their foot while they roll to complete the omoplata.  
  
4. **Kimura**  
- **Counter Submission:**  
- **Straight Armbar**: If you can turn your arm and grip their arm while they attempt the kimura.  
- **Triangle Choke**: If you can isolate their head and arm by using your legs.  
  
5. **Guillotine Choke**  
- **Counter Submission:**  
- **Von Flue Choke**: If you pass to the side control while they hold the guillotine.  
- **Arm Triangle Choke**: If you can control their arm and head from the top position.  
  
6. **Rear Naked Choke (RNC)**  
- **Counter Submission:**  
- **Straight Armbar**: If you can control their arm before they lock the choke.  
- **Triangle Choke**: If you manage to roll and trap their arm with your legs.  
  
7. **Americana**  
- **Counter Submission:**  
- **Kimura**: By reversing the grip and attacking their arm.  
- **Triangle Choke**: If you manage to trap their arm and head with your legs.  
  
8. **Bow and Arrow Choke**  
- **Counter Submission:**  
- **Leg Lock**: As they extend to finish the choke, you can attack their leg.  
- **Reverse Armbar**: If you manage to control their extended arm.  
  
9. **Leg Lock (e.g., Straight Ankle Lock)**  
- **Counter Submission:**  
- **Heel Hook**: By isolating their leg as they attack yours.  
- **Toe Hold**: If you can control their foot during their leg lock attempt.  
  
10. **Darce Choke**  
- **Counter Submission:**  
- **Anaconda Choke**: By reversing the position and attacking their neck.  
- **Guillotine Choke**: If you manage to control their head and neck during their transition.


Clearing full guard
1. **Posture and Base Establishment**:  
- **Posture Up**: Maintain a strong posture by sitting back slightly and keeping your back straight. This makes it harder for your opponent to break your posture and attack.  
- **Grip Control**: Control your opponent’s hips or sleeves to prevent them from executing sweeps or submissions.  
  
2. **Knee in Tailbone (Knee Cutter Pass)**:  
- **Knee in Center**: Position one knee in the centerline of your opponent’s hips, driving it into their tailbone. This helps to open their guard.  
- **Foot Positioning**: Post one foot up on the mat to create leverage.  
- **Breaking the Guard**: Push your opponent’s knee down with your hand or forearm while using your knee to apply pressure on their crossed ankles, encouraging them to open their guard.  
  
3. **Standing Guard Break**:  
- **Grip Control**: Secure a strong grip on your opponent’s gi pants or belt.  
- **Stand Up**: Place one foot on the mat and then stand up, keeping a wide base for balance.  
- **Guard Opening**: Once standing, push down on your opponent’s knee with one hand while maintaining control of their pants with the other, using your weight to break the guard open.  
  
4. **Double Under Pass**:  
- **Underhook Legs**: Reach both arms under your opponent’s legs and clasp your hands together.  
- **Stack Your Opponent**: Lift their hips and stack them on their shoulders, causing discomfort and often opening their guard.  
- **Pass the Guard**: Move to one side while keeping pressure, transitioning to side control.  
  
5. **Can Opener (Caution: Controversial Technique)**:  
- **Head Control**: Grab the back of your opponent’s head with both hands and pull it towards you.  
- **Apply Pressure**: Apply upward pressure on their neck, which can force them to open their guard to relieve the discomfort.  
- **Transition**: Once their guard opens, move quickly to pass.  
  
6. **Combat Base Guard Break**:  
- **Combat Base**: Slide one knee between your opponent’s legs while keeping the other knee up, creating a combat base stance.  
- **Leverage and Pressure**: Use your elbow and forearm to apply pressure on their thigh while using your knee to pry open the guard.  
  
7. **Hip Control and Leg Pin**:  
- **Control Hips**: Place both hands on your opponent’s hips, pushing them down.  
- **Pin Leg**: Step one leg back and then pin your opponent’s leg with your other leg, breaking their guard.
