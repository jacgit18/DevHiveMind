### Armbar Guide

- **Bottom Position (Back Mounted):**
    - If you're already on your back and being pressed, crossing your feet can be helpful for control. However, avoid crossing your feet when you're not stacked, as it can make it easier for your opponent to escape.

- **Bottom Position (Stacked):**
    - When you're stacked, crossing your feet can help control your opponent's posture and make it harder for them to escape.

- **Top Position:**
    - The approach can vary based on the situation, but a common strategy when transitioning to an armbar from the top is to secure an **S-mount**. Grab your opponent's leg to prevent falling off or to disrupt their base.
    - Keeping their base off-balance is key, so focus on control.

- **Hand and Finger Control:**
    - Cover your opponent’s fingers to prevent them from defending or gripping, which could hinder your armbar attempt.

- **Top Position (Armed):**
    - When you're on top and setting up the armbar, you can also consider looping your arms for added control and to improve leverage.

- **Fake a Submission (e.g., Gi Choke):**
    - You can fake a submission like a **gi choke** (or any choke) to get your opponent to react, opening up the armbar from the mount position.
