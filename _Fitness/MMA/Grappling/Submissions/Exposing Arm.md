When you purposefully expose your arm while in the bottom Mount position to bait your opponent into grabbing it, you're setting up traps that capitalize on their commitment to attacking. Here are a few options to reverse the position or transition to a better one:

---

### 1. Trap and Roll Escape  
**Setup**: Once they grab your arm, immediately secure their wrist with your free hand.  

**Execution**:  
1. Use your other arm to hug their trapped arm tightly against your chest.  
2. Simultaneously trap their same-side leg by hooking your foot around their ankle.  
3. Bridge explosively toward the side of their trapped arm to roll them over.  

**Outcome**: You end up in their Guard or top Mount.  

---

### 2. Elbow Escape to Half Guard or Guard  
**Setup**: While they focus on grabbing your arm, use the distraction to shift their weight.  

**Execution**:  
1. Shrimp to create space and slide your knee under their thigh.  
2. Push their knee with your other hand to recover Half Guard or Full Guard.  

**Outcome**: You improve your position, escaping the Mount.  

---

### 3. Arm Drag to Back Take  
**Setup**: When they grab your arm, use their momentum against them.  

**Execution**:  
1. Pull their grabbing arm across your body using an arm drag motion.  
2. Simultaneously hip escape and rotate your body to their back.  

**Outcome**: You move to their back, establishing control for a Rear Naked Choke or another submission.  

---

### 4. Submission Options: Armbar or Triangle Choke  
**Setup**: Bait them deeper into overcommitting to the arm.  

**Execution**:  
1. If they overextend while grabbing your arm, shift your hips and pull their head down with your free arm.  
2. Shoot your legs up to secure a Triangle Choke.  
3. Alternatively, isolate their arm and pivot to attack with an Armbar.  

**Outcome**: You submit them directly from the bottom.  

---

### 5. Bridge and Shrimp into a Sweep  
**Setup**: Time their arm grab to off-balance them.  

**Execution**:  
1. Bridge explosively to shift their weight.  
2. Shrimp out to create an angle and push their hip or thigh with your knee.  
3. Sweep them into a top position.  

**Outcome**: You gain the dominant position.  

---

### **Tips for Success**  

- **Timing and Weight Distribution**: Watch how they shift their weight when grabbing your arm. Use that imbalance to initiate your technique.  
- **Deceptive Baiting**: Make it look like they’ve caught you off-guard when you expose your arm.  
- **Stay Tight**: Avoid giving space that allows them to secure a submission while attacking your bait.  

---

With proper timing and execution, baiting can transition you from a disadvantageous position into a dominant one or set up a submission.
