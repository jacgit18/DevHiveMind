Yes, the principle of chaining **chokes** and **limb attacks** together is true for **Rubber Guard**, **Closed Guard**, and **Butterfly Guard**, and it applies to many other guards as well. This concept is rooted in the idea of creating constant threats and forcing your opponent to defend one attack, which opens opportunities for another. Here's how it works and how it applies to other guards:

---

### **Rubber Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Use setups like the **Dead Orchard** (triangle choke variation) to isolate arms, then transition to an Armbar if they defend. Or, if they defend the Armbar, return to the triangle.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Attack with a **Kimura grip** to force your opponent to expose their neck. Transition into a triangle or omoplata if they counter.

---

### **Closed Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Threaten a cross-collar choke to force their hands up, exposing arms for attacks like the Armbar. If they defend the Armbar, return to collar choke pressure.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Attack with a Kimura or guillotine setup, forcing them to defend by bringing their hands higher. Use this opening to transition into a triangle or back to an Armbar.

---

### **Butterfly Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Use a snap-down or overhook to set up a guillotine. If they defend by extending their arms, transition into an Armbar or sweep.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Attack with a Kimura or Arm Drag to force them to posture or expose their neck. Transition into a guillotine or Darce choke.

---

### **How This Applies to Other Guards**

1. **Half Guard**:
    
    - Attack the **Deep Half Sweep** (limb control), then move to a guillotine (choke).
    - Threaten a Darce choke to force them to retract their arms, setting up an underhook sweep or back take.
2. **De La Riva Guard**:
    
    - Use an Arm Drag (limb attack) to force a reaction and transition to a loop choke.
    - Threaten a collar choke to make them address the grip, opening space for a sweep or leg lock.
3. **Spider Guard**:
    
    - Use the constant tension on the arms to attack a triangle (choke).
    - Transition to an omoplata or Armbar when they posture to defend the triangle.

---

### Why It Works

1. **Human Nature**: Most opponents will instinctively defend the immediate, most dangerous threat. Chokes are often prioritized because they’re seen as more urgent, leaving limbs vulnerable, and vice versa.
2. **Constant Pressure**: By switching between chokes and limb attacks, you maintain control and keep your opponent reactive rather than proactive.
3. **Flow State**: Attacks become fluid rather than isolated, making it harder for the opponent to settle into a defensive rhythm.

This principle is universal to Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu—it encourages chaining techniques and maintaining pressure to overwhelm your opponent. By incorporating it into your guard game, you’ll create more opportunities to finish or improve position!



Here's a breakdown of **choke-to-limb** and **limb-to-choke** sequences for other guards not mentioned earlier:

---

### **Open Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Threaten a cross-collar grip choke while using the distraction to isolate an arm for an Armbar or Omoplata. If they defend the Armbar, transition back to a loop choke.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Use an Arm Drag to force them to defend and expose their neck, then transition to a loop choke or guillotine.

---

### **De La Riva Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Threaten a collar loop choke to force them to posture. As they defend, sweep into a position to attack their arm with an Armbar.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Use a leg drag setup or Arm Drag to pull their posture down and expose their neck for a loop choke or triangle choke.

---

### **Reverse De La Riva Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Use a collar grip to threaten a cross-collar choke. As they defend, transition to an arm attack, such as a Kimura or Wrist Lock.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Attack their exposed arm with an Omoplata, forcing them to posture up and expose their neck for a guillotine or Darce choke.

---

### **Single-Leg X Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Threaten a loop choke or collar choke by pulling their posture down, then transition to an Achilles lock or heel hook when they over-defend.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Attack their leg with a heel hook or straight ankle lock to force a reaction, then transition into a loop choke or triangle if they sit up too aggressively.

---

### **X Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Use a collar grip to pull them into a guillotine or loop choke while attacking a sweep. Transition to an Achilles lock or heel hook once they react.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Sweep or attack a leg to create space, then transition to a triangle choke or loop choke as they posture.

---

### **50/50 Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Use a collar grip to pull them into a choke while isolating their leg for a straight foot lock. Transition back to a choke if they over-defend their leg.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Attack a leg lock to make them focus on their lower body, then pull them into a triangle choke or Darce choke.

---

### **Knee Shield (Z Guard)**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Threaten a cross-collar or loop choke to get them to lift their posture. Transition to a Kimura or Arm Drag when they over-commit to defense.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Attack an Arm Drag or Omoplata, forcing them to retreat, exposing their neck for a guillotine or loop choke.

---

### **Deep Half Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Threaten a Darce choke as you come out of the position. If they defend by exposing an arm, transition to an Americana or Kimura.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Sweep their legs or attack a leg lock to transition into a mounted guillotine or Darce choke.

---

### **Lasso Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Use the lasso to control their posture and threaten a loop choke. If they defend, isolate an arm for an Omoplata or Armbar.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Attack with an Omoplata setup to force them to posture, exposing their neck for a triangle choke or loop choke.

---

### **Spider Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Pull their posture forward using your grips to threaten a triangle choke. If they posture up, transition to an Arm Drag or Omoplata.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Attack the arms using tension from the spider grips to isolate for an Armbar or Omoplata. As they defend, lock in a triangle choke.

---

### **Collar-and-Sleeve Guard**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Use the collar grip to pull them into a loop choke. When they defend, isolate their arm for an Omoplata or triangle choke.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Attack an Arm Drag or wrist control to off-balance them, exposing their neck for a guillotine or loop choke.

---

### **Standing (Guard Pulling or Seated Guard)**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Threaten a guillotine choke while pulling guard, then transition to an Armbar or triangle.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Attack a leg with a sweep to force them to retreat, exposing their neck for a loop choke or guillotine.

---

### **Turtle Guard (Defensive Guard)**

- **Chokes → Limbs → Chokes**: Threaten a guillotine or loop choke while baiting their arms to defend, then isolate their arm for a Kimura.
- **Limbs → Chokes → Limbs**: Attack their arm using a Kimura grip to force a reaction, then transition to a Darce choke or an anaconda choke.

---

This principle applies universally across guards, as chaining **choke** and **limb** threats creates openings, keeps your opponent on the defensive, and ensures continuous pressure in your Jiu-Jitsu game.
