### Real-World Self-Defense: Submission Choices

In self-defense, prioritize submissions based on:

1. **Safety from strikes** (punches, kicks, slams).
2. **Positional control** to limit escapes and counters.
3. **Minimizing exposure** to dangerous counters like being slammed.

### **Bottom Position Submissions**

#### **Full Guard (Closed Guard)**

- **Armbar**: Break posture and isolate the arm.
- **Triangle Choke**: Trap one arm and neck, locking your legs around their head.
- **Kimura**: Secure wrist control for a shoulder lock.
- **Guillotine Choke**: Attack when they expose their neck.
- **Cross Collar Choke**: Deep lapel grips to apply choke pressure.
- **Omoplata**: Shoulder lock via arm isolation.

#### **Open Guards**

- **Spider Guard**:
    - **Triangle Choke**: Control arms, isolate head and neck.
    - **Armbar**: Create openings with grips.
- **De La Riva Guard**:
    - **Triangle Choke**: Set up transitions with off-balancing.
    - **Leg Locks**: Kneebars or ankle locks during sweeps.
- **Butterfly Guard**:
    - **Guillotine Choke**: Against pressure passers.
    - **Armbar**: Opportunities during sweeps.
- **X-Guard**:
    - **Straight Ankle Locks**: Attack leg entanglements.
    - **Heel Hooks**: Focus on leg isolation.

#### **Half Guard**

- **Kimura**: Isolate the arm for a strong shoulder lock.
- **Guillotine Choke**: Attack during forward pressure.
- **Leg Locks**: Kneebars and heel hooks from entanglements.
- **Deep Half Guard**:
    - **Back Takes to RNC**: Transition to the back for rear-naked choke.

#### **Side Control**

- **Buggy Choke**: Pressure when they attempt to strike, trapping head and arm.

#### **Leg Entanglements**

- **Straight Ankle Lock**: Allows distance and targets the lower body without exposing the face.
- **Outside Ashi Garami (Heel Hook)**: Leg farther away for better control.

### **Top Position Submissions**

#### **Passing Full Guard**

- **Arm Triangle**: After passing guard, control the head and arm.
- **Americana**: Attack a pinned arm in dominant positions.
- **Kimura**: Transition from guard pass to side control.

#### **Side Control**

- **Americana (Top or Side Control)**: Trap the arm and apply pressure.
- **Kimura**: Isolate the shoulder with wrist control.
- **Arm Triangle**: Trap head and arm in transition.
- **Baseball Bat Choke**: Use lapel grips for choking pressure.

#### **Mount**

- **Armbar**: Isolate and swing into the submission.
- **Mounted Triangle**: Transition by trapping an arm.
- **Ezekiel Choke**: Sleeve grips for a forearm choke.
- **Cross Collar Choke**: Deep lapel grips for a choke.

#### **Mount/S-Mount/Side Control/Knee-on-Belly/Turtle**

- **Straight Armbar (Mounted or Side Control)**: High control from S-Mount position.

#### **North-South**

- **Kimura**: Leverage isolated arm control.
- **North-South Choke**: Apply choke pressure with head-and-arm control.

#### **Back Mount**

- **Rear Naked Choke (RNC)**: Secure the choke with seatbelt grips.
- **Bow and Arrow Choke**: Attack with lapel grips while controlling the leg.

#### **Leg Entanglements**

- **Straight Ankle Locks**: Attack exposed legs.
- **Heel Hooks**: Apply rotational pressure on the knee.
- **Kneebars**: Isolate and extend the opponent’s leg.

---

### **Key Points to Avoid Dangerous Submissions**

- **Avoid Submissions Exposing You to Strikes**: Don’t attempt **armbars** or **triangles** from closed guard unless confident in controlling posture.
- **Avoid Prolonged Leg Entanglements**: Leg locks (e.g., heel hooks) can be risky if the opponent is mobile.
- **Avoid Sacrificing Position**: Submissions like guillotines or armbars should be applied without pulling guard to avoid slams or strikes.
- **Prioritize Control Before Submission**: Secure control of posture, arms, and head before attempting submissions.

### **Submission Versatility (By Position)**

1. **Armbar (Straight Armbar)**
    
    - **Positions**: 10+ (Mount, S-Mount, Closed Guard, Open Guard, Butterfly Guard, Side Control, Knee-on-Belly, North-South, Back Control, Turtle)
    - **Why Versatile**: Isolates a limb and adapts to nearly any position with arm trapping.
2. **Guillotine Choke**
    
    - **Positions**: 8-10 (Standing, Closed Guard, Butterfly Guard, Half Guard, Open Guard, Mount, Side Control, North-South, Turtle)
    - **Why Versatile**: Can be applied from most positions where neck and posture control are possible.
3. **Rear Naked Choke (RNC)**
    
    - **Positions**: 5-8 (Back Control, Standing, Turtle, Side Control, Mount)
    - **Why Versatile**: Best used for back control but adaptable during transitions.
4. **Triangle Choke**
    
    - **Positions**: 5-7 (Closed Guard, Open Guard, Half Guard, Mount, Side Control, Turtle)
    - **Why Versatile**: Targets both arm and neck, usable in most positions where you can isolate the arm and trap the head.
5. **Kimura**
    
    - **Positions**: 5-6 (Side Control, Mount, Half Guard, Closed Guard, Open Guard, Turtle)
    - **Why Versatile**: Gives control over the shoulder, allowing for transitions to other submissions.
6. **Americana**
    
    - **Positions**: 4-5 (Mount, Side Control, North-South, Half Guard, Knee-on-Belly)
    - **Why Versatile**: Works best when the opponent’s arm is exposed, particularly in tight positions.
7. **Omoplata**
    
    - **Positions**: 4-5 (Closed Guard, Open Guard, Butterfly Guard, Side Control, Turtle)
    - **Why Versatile**: Effective shoulder lock primarily from guard but adaptable to other positions.
8. **Heel Hook**
    
    - **Positions**: 4-5 (Ashi Garami, 50/50 Guard, Open Guard, Half Guard, Butterfly Guard)
    - **Why Versatile**: Leg entanglements make it accessible from both top and bottom positions.
9. **Straight Ankle Lock**
    
    - **Positions**: 4-5 (Ashi Garami, Open Guard, Butterfly Guard, Half Guard, Standing)
    - **Why Versatile**: Common in guard entanglements and effective as a counter during standing exchanges.
10. **Ezekiel Choke**
    

- **Positions**: 3-5 (Mount, Side Control, Half Guard, Back Control, Closed Guard)
- **Why Versatile**: Effective in tight spaces, ideal for gi grips in mount or side control.

11. **Bow and Arrow Choke**

- **Positions**: 3-4 (Back Control, Side Control, Turtle, Mount)
- **Why Versatile**: Uses gi grips, most effective in transitions to back control.

12. **D’Arce Choke**

- **Positions**: 3-4 (Side Control, Turtle, Half Guard, Standing)
- **Why Versatile**: Can be applied when you thread your arm under the neck and far armpit, typically from scrambles.

13. **Peruvian Necktie**

- **Positions**: 3-4 (Turtle, Standing, Side Control, Half Guard)
- **Why Versatile**: A variation of the guillotine choke, effective from front headlock situations.

14. **Wrist Lock**

- **Positions**: 2-4 (Mount, Closed Guard, Side Control, Standing)
- **Why Versatile**: Requires minimal positioning but opportunistic and rare compared to other submissions.

15. **Arm Triangle (Head and Arm Choke)**

- **Positions**: 3-4 (Mount, Side Control, Half Guard, Closed Guard)
- **Why Versatile**: Effective in top positions with control over arm and neck.

16. **Calf Slicer**

- **Positions**: 2-3 (Half Guard, Turtle, Open Guard)
- **Why Versatile**: Rare but effective from leg entanglements.

---

### **General Trends**

- **Most Versatile**: Armbars, guillotines, and triangles are dominant and can be used across nearly every position.
- **Least Versatile**: Calf slicers and Peruvian neckties are more position-specific and require precise setups.
