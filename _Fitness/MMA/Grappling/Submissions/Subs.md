In real-world self-defense, your submission choices should prioritize:

1. **Safety from strikes (punches, kicks, slams).**
2. **Positional control to limit escapes and counters.**
3. **Minimizing exposure to dangerous counters like being slammed.**
### **Bottom Position Submissions**

#### **Full Guard (Closed Guard)**
- **Armbar**: Break posture and isolate the arm.
- **Triangle Choke**: Trap one arm and the neck, locking your legs around their head.
- **Kimura**: Secure wrist control and overhook for a shoulder lock.
- **Guillotine Choke**: Attack when the opponent exposes their neck or leans forward.
- **Cross Collar Choke**: Use deep lapel grips to apply choking pressure.
- **Omoplata**: Transition to a shoulder lock by attacking the arm.

#### **Open Guards**
- **Spider Guard**:
    - **Triangle Choke**: Control arms and isolate the head and neck.
    - **Armbar**: Manipulate grips to create submission openings.
- **De La Riva Guard**:
    - **Triangle Choke**: Off-balance and set up choke transitions.
    - **Leg Locks**: Attack kneebars or ankle locks during sweeps.
- **Butterfly Guard**:
    - **Guillotine Choke**: Effective against pressure passers.
    - **Armbar**: Openings appear during sweeps.
- **X-Guard**:
    - **Straight Ankle Locks**: Attack leg entanglements with ankle locks.
    - **Heel Hooks**: Focus on leg isolation in transitions.

#### **Half Guard (Including Variations)**
- **Kimura**: Isolate the arm and control posture for a strong shoulder lock.
- **Guillotine Choke**: Attack during forward pressure from your opponent.
- **Leg Locks**: Kneebars and heel hooks are effective from entanglements.
- **Deep Half Guard**:
    - **Back Takes to RNC**: Transition to the back for rear-naked choke submissions.

#### **Side Control**
- **Buggy Choke**: Apply pressure when opponents attempt to strike, trapping their head and arm. **(Safety Tip)**: Transition quickly if needed to avoid strikes.

#### **Leg Entanglements (Bottom/Guard Passing)**
- **Straight Ankle Lock**: Allows distance and targets the lower body without exposing your face to strikes.
- **Outside Ashi Garami (Heel Hook)**: Keeps the other leg farther away for better control and safety.

### **Top Position Submissions**

#### **Passing Full Guard**
- **Arm Triangle**: Control the head and arm after passing guard.
- **Americana**: Attack a pinned arm once in a dominant position.
- **Kimura (From Guard or Side Control)**:  Transition from passing guard into side control for the submission.

#### **Side Control**
- **Americana (Top Mount or Side Control)**: Trap the arm and apply pressure toward the head.
- **Kimura**: Use wrist control to isolate the shoulder.
- **Arm Triangle**: Trap the opponent’s head and arm during a transition.
- **Baseball Bat Choke**: Use lapel grips to create choke pressure.

#### **Mount**
- **Armbar**: Swing into the submission after isolating an arm.
- **Mounted Triangle**: Transition by trapping an arm and wrapping your legs.
- **Ezekiel Choke**: Use sleeve grips to apply a forearm choke.
- **Cross Collar Choke**: Utilize deep lapel grips for a choke.

#### Mount/S-Mount/Side Control/Knee-on-Belly/Turtle
S-mount is a high variation of the mount position where you elevate your knee above the opponent's head. The position allows for greater control and better access to submissions.

- **Straight Armbar (Mounted or Side Control)**

#### **North-South**
- **Kimura**: Leverage isolated arm control.
- **North-South Choke**: Apply choke pressure with head-and-arm control.

#### **Back Mount**
- **Rear Naked Choke (RNC)**: Use seatbelt grips and secure the choke.
- **Bow and Arrow Choke**: Attack with lapel grips while controlling the leg.

#### **Leg Entanglements (Top/Guard Passing)**
- **Straight Ankle Locks**: Attack exposed legs.
- **Heel Hooks**: Apply rotational pressure on the knee.
- **Kneebars**: Isolate and extend the opponent’s leg for the submission.

### **Key Points to Avoid Dangerous Submissions:**

- **Avoid Submissions That Expose You to Strikes:**
    - Don’t attempt **armbars** or **triangles** from closed guard unless you’re confident in your ability to control their posture.
    
- **Avoid Prolonged Leg Entanglements:**
    - Leg locks (e.g., heel hooks) can be risky if the opponent is still mobile enough to kick.

- **Avoid Pulling Guard or Sacrificing Position:**
    - Submissions like guillotines or armbars should be applied without pulling guard, as this may result in slams or strikes.

- **Prioritize Control Before Submission:**
    - Always secure control of the opponent’s posture, arms, and head before going for a submission.

### **Summary**

- **Bottom Position**: Focus on sweeps transitioning into submissions like armbars, triangles, and guillotines. Open guards emphasize defensive setups and offensive attacks.
- **Top Position**: Emphasize control after passing guard, targeting arm locks, chokes, and leg locks.
