In Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ), most of the **stand-up takedown game**—especially when it’s **not judo-based**—draws heavily from **wrestling**, particularly **freestyle** and **folkstyle** wrestling. Here are the main components of takedowns in BJJ that come from wrestling:

---

### **1. Double Leg Takedown**

- Classic wrestling move.
- Lower your level, penetrate, clasp both legs, and drive.
- Common in both gi and no-gi BJJ, especially no-gi.

### **2. Single Leg Takedown**

- Another wrestling staple.
- Control one leg, lift, and finish with a variety of trips or dumps.
- Very popular in BJJ due to its adaptability.

### **3. Body Lock Takedowns**

- Common in Greco-Roman and folkstyle wrestling.
- Over-under or double underhooks leading to trips, mat returns, or throws.
- Useful in no-gi and even in some gi scenarios.

### **4. Snap Downs to Front Headlock**

- Classic wrestling tactic.
- Break posture, pull the head down, look for guillotines, go-behinds, or anaconda chokes.
- Great setup for submissions or positional control in BJJ.


### **5. High Crotch / Crackdown**

- Similar to a single leg but with the head on the inside.
- Common entry point to lift and finish or transition to other positions.

### **6. Sprawling and Takedown Defense**

- Sprawl-and-spin or sprawl-to-front-headlock are wrestling-based defensive tactics.
- Critical in BJJ, especially in no-gi, where defending a shot is key.


### **7. Chain Wrestling / Scrambling**

- Wrestling brings the idea of **continual movement**—if one takedown fails, transition to another.
- This mentality has deeply influenced high-level no-gi grappling.


### **8. Mat Returns**

- Lifting or tripping an opponent who’s standing up from turtle or trying to escape.
- Very wrestling-heavy, often seen after back takes or failed submission attempts.

---

### **Bonus: Collar Ties and Hand Fighting**

- Fundamental in wrestling for setting up shots.
- Also used heavily in BJJ to control posture, open up entries, or frustrate guard pullers.
