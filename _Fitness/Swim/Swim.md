When it comes to swimming, the type of kick you use often depends on the stroke, your goals, and the level of intensity you’re aiming for. Here's a breakdown of different swimming forms and the preferred kick patterns:  
  
### **Freestyle (Front Crawl)**  
- **Six-Beat Kick:** This is the most common and involves six kicks per stroke cycle (three kicks per arm stroke). It’s ideal for longer distances and competitive swimming as it provides consistent propulsion and helps maintain a streamlined body position.  
- **Two-Beat Kick:** This involves two kicks per stroke cycle (one kick per arm stroke) and is typically used in long-distance or open-water swimming. It's less tiring and helps conserve energy while maintaining a steady pace.  
  
### **Backstroke**  
- **Six-Beat Kick:** Similar to freestyle, this kick is used for backstroke to maintain speed and balance. It helps keep the body aligned and provides propulsion.  
- **Two-Beat Kick:** Less common in backstroke but can be used for long-distance swimming to conserve energy.  
  
### **Breaststroke**  
- **No Kick Pattern (Frog Kick):** The breaststroke uses a completely different kick pattern called the frog kick or whip kick. The legs move simultaneously in a circular motion, and there is no relation to two or six-beat kicks.  
  
### **Butterfly**  
- **Two-Beat Dolphin Kick:** The butterfly stroke traditionally uses a two-beat dolphin kick where the legs move in unison and kick twice per stroke cycle. This kick helps in maintaining the rhythm and timing of the stroke.  
- **Six-Beat Dolphin Kick:** While less common, some swimmers may use more frequent kicks in shorter sprints to maintain higher speed, but it requires a lot of energy.  
  
### **Doggy Paddle**  
- **Freestyle (Flutter) Kick or No Kick Pattern:** The doggy paddle is typically a relaxed stroke often used for leisurely swimming or treading water. It usually involves a freestyle kick, which can be done at any pace or pattern, depending on comfort. There's no strict kick pattern associated with it.  
  
### **Sidestroke**  
- **Scissor Kick:** The sidestroke uses a unique scissor kick where the legs move in opposite directions. This kick provides gentle propulsion and is good for long-distance or rescue swimming where energy conservation is key.  
  
### **Treading Water**  
- **Eggbeater Kick:** When treading water, the eggbeater kick is most effective. This involves a circular motion of the legs in opposite directions, allowing for steady buoyancy and minimal effort.  
  
### **Summary of When to Use Each Kick:**  
- **Six-Beat Kick:** Ideal for competitive, high-speed swimming in freestyle and backstroke.  
- **Two-Beat Kick:** Best for long-distance, open-water swimming, or when conserving energy is crucial.  
- **No Kick Pattern:** For strokes like breaststroke, butterfly, or doggy paddle that have specific kicking motions not related to the two or six-beat patterns.  
  
The choice between a two-beat and six-beat kick largely depends on your goals (speed vs. endurance) and the specific stroke you’re swimming.
