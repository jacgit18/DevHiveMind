# Circling Inside or Outside the Arms in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ)

In Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ), the choice of circling inside or outside your opponent's arms depends on the goals of the position and situation. Unlike striking, where circling outside is generally safer to avoid attacks, BJJ allows more flexibility, as both inside and outside control can be advantageous based on the leverage or submission opportunities you're pursuing.

---

## **Circling Inside the Arm in BJJ**

### **Advantages**

Circling inside the arm is effective for gaining inside control, which is essential for setting up grips, frames, underhooks, or dominant positions. Examples include:

- **Underhooks**  
    Circling inside allows you to dig for underhooks, one of the most powerful tools for controlling your opponent in positions like half guard, the standing clinch, or side control transitions.
    
- **Collar Ties**  
    From the inside, you can establish strong grips to control your opponent’s posture, set up sweeps, or initiate takedowns and submissions.
    
- **Submission Setups**  
    Circling inside creates openings for submissions such as armbars, triangle chokes, or limb isolation (especially in gi-based techniques).
    

---

### **Risks**

Circling inside can leave you vulnerable to counters, including:

- Overhooks, which can neutralize your control and lead to sweeps or submissions.
- Guillotine attempts, particularly if you allow your head position to drop below the opponent's.
- Wrist control, which can restrict your movement and lead to setups like kimuras or armlocks.

Proper head positioning, posture, and alignment are critical to mitigating these risks when circling inside.

---

## **Circling Outside the Arm in BJJ**

### **Advantages**

Circling outside is a safer strategy when you need to maintain distance, break grips, or disengage. It's particularly effective for avoiding being tied up or countered. Examples include:

- **Grip Fighting**  
    Circling outside helps you break your opponent’s grips while minimizing the risk of direct confrontation. This strategy is especially useful in gi-based grappling, where grip control is crucial.
    
- **Guard Passing**  
    Circling outside minimizes the risk of getting caught in your opponent's frames or entangled in their legs during guard passing.
    
- **Maintaining Safety**  
    When in doubt or when facing aggressive opponents, circling outside allows you to stay in a safer position, particularly during transitions or scrambles.
    

---

### **Similarities to Striking**

Circling outside in BJJ shares many parallels with striking in terms of maintaining safety and avoiding direct threats. In striking, circling outside your opponent’s arms is often preferred because it keeps you:

- Out of range of punches, elbows, and other strikes.
- Away from dangerous counters like hooks or uppercuts when closing the distance.
- Positioned at an angle where you can set up your own attacks without exposing yourself to direct retaliation.

In contrast, **circling inside the arms in striking is typically risky** because it places you closer to your opponent's power and attack range. Some of the main risks include:

- **Getting Hit by Straight Punches or Uppercuts**  
    Moving inside gives your opponent a direct line to attack, especially with their lead hand or combinations.
    
- **Exposing Yourself to Clinch Attacks**  
    Circling inside without proper control often results in getting tied up in a clinch, where your opponent can land knees, elbows, or set up throws.
    
- **Vulnerability to Angled Strikes**  
    Inside positioning can make it harder to defend against strikes like hooks or overhands, as you're within the arc of their attack.
    

In BJJ, these striking risks don't exist as prominently, which is why circling inside is often a goal rather than a danger. However, if you lack proper posture, head positioning, or awareness, even in grappling, circling inside can expose you to counters like overhooks, guillotines, or sweeps.

---

## **Key Takeaway**

In BJJ, circling inside or outside depends on your specific goal and the situation. The primary difference from striking is that **inside control is often desirable in BJJ**, allowing you to dominate grips, posture, and positioning. However, just as in striking, **circling outside offers safety** and is ideal for maintaining distance, avoiding grips, and disengaging. Your choice should depend on:

1. The type of control you wish to establish.
2. Your ability to defend against potential counters.
3. The level of risk you're willing to take in a given position or exchange.
