# 🧘‍♂️ **15-Minute Mobility Routine**

A **comprehensive routine** combining **foam rolling, lacrosse ball, and Swiss ball** work for **pain relief, rehab, and functional movement prep**.  
Adjust intensity based on your goals: _maintenance vs. injury recovery_.

---

## **1. Foam Rolling (5–6 Minutes)**

**🎯 Goal:** Release large muscle groups and fascial tension  
**🛠️ Technique:** Slow rolls (1 inch/sec) + pause on tender spots (5–10 sec)

### **A. Lower Body**

- **Quads (1 min):** Lie facedown, roll mid-thigh to knee.  
    _Tip:_ Bend knee for deeper pressure.
    
- **Hamstrings (1 min):** Sit with legs extended, roll glutes to knees.  
    _Tip:_ Rotate legs inward/outward.
    
- **Calves (1 min):** Cross one leg over the other, roll from Achilles to knee.
    
- **IT Band (30 sec/side):** Lie on side, roll hip to knee.  
    _Note:_ Keep brief — avoid overstimulation.
    

### **B. Upper Body**

- **Lats (30 sec/side):** Lie on side, arm overhead, roll armpit to mid-back.
    
- **Thoracic Spine (1 min):** Roll upper/mid-back with hands behind head.  
    _Tip:_ Extend over roller to open chest.
    

---

## **2. Lacrosse Ball (4–5 Minutes)**

**🎯 Goal:** Target trigger points and smaller, deeper muscles  
**🛠️ Technique:** 30–45 sec per spot, use gentle circles or static pressure

### **A. Feet / Plantar Fascia (1 min/side):**

- Stand or sit, roll under arch and heel.  
    _Excellent for plantar fasciitis._
    

### **B. Glutes / Piriformis (1 min/side):**

- Sit on ball, cross ankle over opposite knee, lean into tight spots.
    

### **C. Upper Back / Shoulders (1 min/side):**

- Pin ball between spine and wall, target rhomboids and rear delts.
    

### **D. Pec Minor (30 sec/side):**

- Place ball below collarbone, lean into doorway.  
    _Helps fix rounded shoulders._
    

---

## **3. Swiss Ball (4–5 Minutes)**

**🎯 Goal:** Core activation, spine mobility, and stability

### **A. Hip Flexor Stretch (1 min/side):**

- Kneel with one leg on ball, lunge forward.  
    _Stretch plus balance challenge._
    

### **B. Swiss Ball Wall Squats (1 min):**

- Place ball between lower back and wall, squat slowly.  
    _Engages glutes and quads._
    

### **C. Thoracic Extensions (1 min):**

- Lie back on ball, arms behind head, gently extend backward.  
    _Great posture reset._
    

### **D. Dead Bug (1 min):**

- Lie back on ball, alternate extending opposite arms and legs.  
    _Core stability and control._
    

---

## ✅ **Routine Summary**

|**Tool**|**Focus Area**|**Time**|**Key Benefit**|
|---|---|---|---|
|Foam Roller|Lower/Upper Body|5–6 min|Myofascial release|
|Lacrosse Ball|Trigger Points|4–5 min|Deep pain relief|
|Swiss Ball|Mobility / Core|4–5 min|Dynamic stability + posture|

🕒 **Total Time:** ~15 Minutes

---

## 💡 **Pro Tips**

- **Rehab Mode:** Use lighter pressure; avoid pain flare-ups.
    
- **Pre-Workout:** Prioritize dynamic work (e.g., Swiss ball squats).
    
- **Post-Workout:** Spend extra time on chronically tight areas (e.g., lats, hamstrings).
    

---

**Do this 3x/week** for noticeable mobility and pain reduction.

# **🚫 What _Not_ to Do During Foam Rolling, Lacrosse Ball, and Swiss Ball Routines**

Here’s a **clear list of what NOT to touch or do** during your foam rolling, lacrosse ball, and Swiss ball routine, based on anatomy and injury risks:

---

## **🚫 Foam Rolling: Avoid These Areas**

1. **Lower Back (Lumbar Spine):**
    
    - _Why?_ Rolling directly on vertebrae can compress discs and irritate nerves.
        
    - _Fix:_ Roll **upper back (thoracic)** only, or use a Swiss ball for gentle lumbar mobility.
        
2. **IT Band (Direct Rolling):**
    
    - _Why?_ The IT band is dense fascia—rolling it aggressively can inflame it further.
        
    - _Fix:_ Focus on muscles _around_ it (quads, hamstrings, glutes) to relieve tension.
        
3. **Neck (Front or Sides):**
    
    - _Why?_ Risk of compressing arteries/nerves (e.g., carotid artery).
        
    - _Fix:_ Use hands or a **peanut ball** for gentle neck releases _behind the skull_.
        
4. **Knees/Elbows (Bony Areas):**
    
    - _Why?_ No muscle to roll—just pain and potential bursitis.
        
    - _Fix:_ Roll **above/below joints** (e.g., quads for knees, triceps for elbows).
        

---

## **🚫 Lacrosse Ball: Danger Zones**

1. **Spine (Vertebrae):**
    
    - _Why?_ Direct pressure can misalign or compress spinal joints.
        
    - _Fix:_ Place ball **beside the spine** (targeting erector muscles).
        
2. **Kidneys (Lower Ribs/Back):**
    
    - _Why?_ Organs lack protection here—bruising risk.
        
    - _Fix:_ Stay above the bottom rib when rolling upper back.
        
3. **Armpit (Axilla):**
    
    - _Why?_ Nerves/lymph nodes live here.
        
    - _Fix:_ Roll **pec minor** (below collarbone) or **lats** (mid-back) instead.
        
4. **Front of Shin (Tibia):**
    
    - _Why?_ Thin tissue over bone—rolling can cause bruising.
        
    - _Fix:_ Use hands for shin massage or roll **calf muscles** to relieve tension.
        

---

## **🚫 Swiss Ball: Risky Moves**

1. **Hyperextending the Neck:**
    
    - _Why?_ Craning neck during exercises (e.g., crunches) strains cervical spine.
        
    - _Fix:_ Keep chin slightly tucked during ab work.
        
2. **Unsupported Lumbar Flexion:**
    
    - _Why?_ Rolling backward over ball can overstretch lower back ligaments.
        
    - _Fix:_ Use ball for **gentle extensions only** (stop if you feel compression).
        
3. **Standing on the Ball:**
    
    - _Why?_ High fall risk unless you’re a circus performer.
        
    - _Fix:_ Stick to seated/lying exercises for stability.
        

---

## **⚠️ General No-Nos for All Tools**

1. **Rolling Over Acute Injuries:**
    
    - Swollen/sprained areas need **RICE (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation)**, not pressure.
        
2. **Numbness/Tingling:**
    
    - _Why?_ Sign of nerve compression—stop immediately.
        
3. **Excessive Pressure on Bones:**
    
    - Femur, ribs, and pelvis are **not muscle**—rolling them does nothing but hurt.
        
4. **Holding Breath:**
    
    - Tension reduces effectiveness—breathe deeply, especially on tight spots.
        

---

## **✅ Safe Alternatives for Problem Areas**

|**Area to Avoid**|**Safer Alternative**|
|---|---|
|Lower Back|Swiss ball pelvic tilts|
|IT Band|Foam roll quads/glutes|
|Front of Shin|Calf rolling + manual massage|
|Neck|Peanut ball suboccipital release|

---

**Pro Tip:** If you feel **sharp pain** (not dull discomfort), you’re either:

- On a **nerve or bone** → _Move off the area._
    
- Too **aggressive** → _Lighten pressure._
