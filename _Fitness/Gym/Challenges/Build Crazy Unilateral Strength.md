# Single-Arm Push-Up Progression Plan

Building up to a **single-arm push-up** requires a strategic blend of *strength progression*, *stability work*, and *technique drills*.  
Since you can already do decent push-ups, your foundation is solid — but this move demands **3× more core stability, shoulder control, and triceps strength** than regular push-ups.

---

## Phase 1: Build Foundational Strength (2–4 Weeks)
*Target: Strengthen weak links*

1. **Uneven Push-Ups**  
   - One hand on a *book/block* (3–4" high), the other on the floor.  
   - 3× 8–10 reps/side.  
   - *Goal:* Shift 70% weight to the lower hand.

2. **Archer Push-Ups**  
   - Wide hand position; bend one arm while keeping the other straight.  
   - Lower until chest touches bent arm’s wrist.  
   - 3× 5–8 reps/side.

3. **Weighted Push-Ups**  
   - Wear a backpack with books/weights.  
   - 3× 6–10 reps.

---

## Phase 2: Master Stability & Partial Range (2–3 Weeks)
*Target: Core anti-rotation + controlled descent*

1. **Elevated Single-Arm Push-Ups**  
   - Hands on a *bench/table* (waist height).  
   - Shift weight to one arm, cross free foot behind ankle.  
   - Lower slowly for 3–5 seconds, **stop halfway down**.  
   - 3× 3–5 reps/side.

2. **Pallof Press (Anti-Rotation Core)**  
   - Band anchored at chest height.  
   - Extend arms straight out, resisting band pull. Hold 20–30 sec/side.  
   - *Why:* Prevents torso collapse during single-arm push-ups.

3. **Scapular Push-Ups**  
   - Plank → protract shoulders (push upper back toward ceiling) → retract (pull blades together).  
   - 2× 15 reps.

---

## Phase 3: Full Technique & Overcoming Fear (Ongoing)

1. **Band-Assisted Single-Arm Push-Ups**  
   - Resistance band around waist, anchored above (e.g., pull-up bar).  
   - Reduces body weight by 20–40%.  
   - 3× 1–3 reps/side.

2. **Negative Single-Arm Push-Ups**  
   - Start at the top position (one arm, feet wide).  
   - Lower slowly (5–8 seconds) to the floor.  
   - Reset. 3× 2–3 reps/side.

3. **Floor-Assisted Single-Arm Push-Ups**  
   - Free hand on *small ball/water bottle* for balance only.  
   - Gradually reduce pressure until ready to lift it entirely.

---

## Critical Form Cues
- **Feet Position**: Wider than shoulder-width (more stability).  
- **Core**: Brace as if about to be punched (prevents hip sag).  
- **Shoulder**: Screw working hand into the floor (engage lats).  
- **Elbow**: Keep at 45° to ribs (avoid flaring).

> 🛡️ **Fear Fix**: Place a folded towel/pillow under your face initially. Practice on grass/carpet until confident.

---

## Sample Weekly Progression Plan

| Day 1 (Strength) | Day 2 (Stability) | Day 3 (Technique) |
|------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Weighted Push-Ups | Elevated Partial Reps | Band-Assisted Singles |
| Archer Push-Ups   | Pallof Press         | Negatives           |
| Uneven Push-Ups   | Plank w/ Arm Lift    | Core Activation     |

**Rest 1–2 days between sessions.** Test unassisted reps every 3 weeks.

---

## Why This Works
- **Strengthens the sticking point**: Negatives/partials target the hardest eccentric phase.  
- **Fixes imbalances**: Uneven/archer push-ups reveal and train weaker sides.  
- **Builds push-up–specific core stability**: Pallof press trains anti-rotation.

> 💡 **If your arm collapses inward**: Add *band pull-aparts* (3× 20 daily) to strengthen rear delts and rotator cuffs — weakness here often causes elbow flare.

---

**Timeline:** Expect your first clean single-arm push-up in **8–12 weeks** with consistent training.  
Be patient — this is a top-tier bodyweight skill! 💪
