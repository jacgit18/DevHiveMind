# Striking Guide
![[sced.jpg]]
### **Practice & Simulation**

Use the **UFC game** to simulate and experiment with techniques and strategies.

## **Striking Principles**

### **1. Be Playful and Adaptive**
- Treat sparring as a game; keep it light and exploratory.
- Imagine the opponent as a mirror: move in the opposite direction of their movement, emulating **lateral inversion** which the process of your movement in a mirror orienting inversely.
- When exiting or entering feel the void of space with something don't be a good guess and bring a gift when you come and leave showing manners giving some hands as you exit.
- Push pace while being defensively responsible also using boxing against Muay Thai heavy attacker. 

**Stance Basics**:
- **Orthodox**: Lead with your left foot and hand. ^3c7c38
- **Southpaw**: Lead with your right foot and hand. ^42f230


### **2. Defense & Punch Timing**
- **Defensive Hand Positioning**:
    - Use your backhand to catch and block punches.
    - Keep your backhand higher to protect your head and as your primary guard hand alternating between guarding with the one and both.
    - You can keep your lead hand lower for flexibility and speed.
- **Punch Timing**: Aim to strike between heartbeats for precision and flow.


### **3. Striking & Fainting**

- Use the **teep kick** like a jab to control range or disrupt rhythm, especially against taller opponents.
- **Sneaky Kicks**: Avoid telegraphing by minimizing shoulder movement.
- **Faint Front Kick**: Lift your leg and show the sole of your foot to sell the feint, setting up other strikes.
- **Faint Spin Kick**: faint the spin kick and doing a stance switch to a front or rear kick.
-  **Faint Body/Head**: punch or kick to the body or head then attack the other target.

### **4. Rhythm & Movement**
- **Play with Rhythm**:
    - Move in and out of rhythm with different music.
    - Throw strikes in the middle of a rhythm, break rhythm, or return to it.
    - Try the **Thai hop** during rhythm shifts to confuse your opponent.
    - Throw shots you want your opponent to throw so if you want them to jab do a jab.
- **Stance Switching**:
    - Switch stances at various points—before, during, or after rhythm.
    - Experiment to discover what feels natural and unpredictable.


### **5. Wall Leaning Strategy**
- Use the wall to limit your movement intentionally.
- This tactic can bait the opponent to attack, creating openings for counters.


### **6. Unpredictability & Adaptation**
- Opponents adapt after successful strikes or combos. Stay one step ahead by:
    - **Varying Techniques**: Throw strikes with incomplete technique or delayed timing.
    - **Observing Reactions**: Watch how they adjust to your attacks.
    - **Mixing Angles and Setups**: Alter the angle, setup, or rhythm of your strikes.
- Keep your attacks unpredictable to force your opponent into constant reassessment.

By continuously experimenting and refining your approach, you'll develop a dynamic striking style that keeps opponents guessing.


## **Stance**

### **1. Bladed vs. Squared Stance**
- **Bladed Stance**:
    - Ideal for evasion and defensive movement.
    - Reduces your target area and allows for faster lateral movement.
- **Squared Stance**:
    - Best for offense, with your belly button facing the opponent.
    - Enables quicker and more powerful kicks.
    - Makes it easier to catch body kicks due to better alignment with the opponent’s strikes.

### **2. Narrow vs. Wide Stance**
- **Tight, Close-Foot Stance**:
    - Harder for opponents to read your movements.
    - Enhances feints and disguises your strikes, keeping your attacks unpredictable.
- **Wide Stance**:
    - Offers stability and balance for power strikes but may telegraph movements more easily.

Switch between stances strategically to adapt to the situation and maintain an unpredictable approach.

## **Distance Management**

### **Circling**
When you circle, do so with intent, keeping your movement slow and controlled as you close the distance. This allows you to gauge your opponent's reaction and stay unpredictable. The key is to not rush; instead, use the circular movement to naturally draw your opponent into the range where your strikes are most effective.

Keep your head moving as you circle. This not only makes you harder to hit, but it also sets you up to throw quick, reactive strikes once you're in range. The head movement distracts and disrupts your opponent's timing, making it harder for them to predict your next move.

Rather than stopping your movement and then launching an attack, use the momentum of your circling to flow smoothly into your strikes. This creates a more natural, fluid offense and makes it harder for your opponent to predict when and where the attack is coming. Your opponent may be more likely to react late because you're maintaining motion and fluidity.

You can mix in brief pauses where you stop circling, then explode with a strike. This change in tempo disrupts their rhythm and throws off their timing, making it harder for them to react to your attacks. The sudden stop can trick them into thinking you're out of range, only to strike when they’re not expecting it.

The combination of flowing and pausing keeps your opponent guessing. Use the momentum of circling for a consistent offensive threat but intersperse occasional stops and strikes to confuse them, causing hesitation and opening up counter opportunities for you.

### **Hand Fighting**
- **Too Close**: If their wrist is bending during hand fighting, they are too close.
- **Control & Redirect**:
    - Push their arm from the outside inward, starting at the forearm.
    - Progress control to the elbow, shoulder, or even their chest for maximum leverage.
    - Avoid pushing the inside arm, as this opens you up to counters.
    - Frame their arm off your jab hand to disrupt their rhythm and maintain control.

### **Guard Transitions**
- **Long Guard**: Use at long range to control distance and deflect strikes.
- **Cross Guard**: Employ at close range for tighter defense and quick counters.
- **Philly Shell or Modified Shell**:
    - Use at close range for shoulder-based defense and counters.
    - Rely on shoulder rolls and precise movement to deflect strikes.

### **Distance Techniques**
- **Kick to the Shin**: In a street context, disrupt their balance and keep distance with a shin kick.
- **Focus on the Waist**: The waist reveals movement intentions more accurately than the upper body or eyes.
- **Angle Creation**:
    - Establish the rhythm first, then break it to create angles and fire off strikes.
    - Attack from unexpected positions to stay unpredictable.

### **Avoiding Strong Stances**
- Limit or avoid engaging when your opponent is in a strong stance to reduce the risk of counters.
- If you can’t see their punch, move in the opposite direction of their striking hand to create space and avoid the attack.


## **Counter Striking Guide**

### **Opposite Stance (Opposite Lead Foot)**
- This occurs when you and your opponent have opposite lead feet (e.g., you're [[Striking Approach#^3c7c38 |Orthodox]], and they're [[Striking Approach#^42f230 |Southpaw]]). It's generally easier to step outside their lead foot.

- Consider different Options listed here [[Orthodox vs Southpaw]]

#### **Key Techniques**

1. **Catch and Return**:
    - Catch their jab with your backhand and immediately return a jab.
    - Use half-timing to disrupt their rhythm. For example:
        - Jab over or under their jab to create openings.

2. **Rolling Counters**:
    - Roll blocking with shoulder and counter counter, but avoid doing so on the same side.
    - Utilize low-hand counters if available.

3. **Brush and Counter**:
    - Swat the punch away with your backhand or lead hand, then:
        - Return a jab.
        - Throw a hook if they are taller.
        - Use an uppercut if they are shorter (adjust to stance accordingly).

4. **Slip and Open**:
    - Slip punches to create openings for uppercuts.

5. **Pull Counters**:
    - Lean back to pull their strike out of range and counter as they retract.

6. **Sidesteps**:
    - Step laterally to the outside of their lead foot to create angles for counterattacks.

### **Same Stance (Same Lead Foot)**
- Occurs when you and your opponent have the same lead foot (e.g., both Orthodox or both Southpaw). It’s harder to step outside their lead foot, and leverage dynamics differ.

#### **Key Techniques**
1. **Catching and Slapping**:
    - Catch their jab and slap down their lead hand.
    
1. **Block and Shoot**:
    - To counter leverage issues, block slightly outward instead of  blocking  with arms tight to head or body.
    - Follow up with precise counters.

2. **Brush and Counter**:
    - Swat their punch away (with lead or backhand), then counter based on their height and stance:
        - Jab.
        - Hook (taller opponents).
        - Uppercut (shorter opponents).

3. **Southpaw Techniques**:
    - Control their lead hand during hand fighting, push it down, and counter.
    - Feint high or low after hand control to open up strikes.

### **Same-Time Counters**
These counters require precise timing and coordination, as they are harder to execute but highly effective.

1. **Block and Strike Simultaneously**:
    - Block their attack while throwing a jab simultaneously.
    
2. **Jab Slip**:
    - Slip their punch while jabbing. Be cautious, as this can leave you vulnerable to a cross.

3. **Lead Hand Control**:    
    - In Southpaw, control their lead hand, step to the side, or cross block to create openings.
    
4. **Leg Kick Counter**:
    - Throw an outside leg kick when they jab to disrupt their balance.

### **Inside Counters**
Focus on tight, compact strikes when they throw wide punches.

1. **Tight and Guarded**:
    - Stay tight and guard while throwing compact strikes, leveraging timing and half-timing to exploit their openings.

2. **Shoulder Integration**:
    - Use your shoulder to generate power in counters, especially when throwing inside strikes.

## Technique Mechanics

- **Lead Hook Mechanics:** Rotate on your back foot when throwing a standalone lead hook. Only rotate on your front leg when throwing a combination that includes a lead hook for better weight transition.
    
- **Pivoting Technique:** Begin with your back heel lifting and pivoting for balance, then transition by lifting your front heel to pivot for the lead hook. This ensures fluid motion and power transfer.
    
- **Check Hook Variation:** For a check hook, pivot off your front foot without transitioning between feet. This method prioritizes speed and angle creation, allowing for a quick counter with a slight fade-away or lean to evade the opponent’s attack.
